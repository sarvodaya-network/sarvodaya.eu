# Ethics of the Dhammapada 

Vinoba said:  
> Dhammapad outlines the science of ethics. Dhammapad is the best as far as ethics is concerned. It can generate spiritual hunger. That is its purpose. The Gita begins where the Dhammapad ends. (Reflections on the Gita)

He himself has published a re-arranged version of the Dhammapada, which sadly has never been translated, as far as I am informed; I am therefore not able to study it. Nonetheless, we can refer to the original Dhammapada in its unordered form and will find the ethics therein that Vinoba is referring to and considers to be "the best".

## Condensing CHAPTER I:

> Phenomena are preceded by the mind, ruled by the mind, made of the mind.  
> If you speak or act with a corrupted mind, suffering follows you.  
> If you speak or act with a calm, bright mind, happiness follows you.  

> Hostility isn't stilled for those who brood on the Wrong that others did to them. 
> Hostility is stilled for those who don't brood on the Wrong that others did to them. 

> Hostilities aren't stilled through hostility.  
> Hostilities are stilled through non-hostility.  
> this is an eternal truth. 

> Stay focused on the foul
> Be restrained with regard to the senses
> Know moderation in food
> full of conviction & energy;
> then Mara does not overcome you.

> Be free of depravity
> endowed with truthfulness & self-control 
> well-established in the precepts.  

> Know essence as essence and non-essence as non-  
> get to the essence, ranging about in right resolves. 

> Passion does seep into the well-developed mind.  

> The wrong-doer grieves. He grieves, he's afflicted, seeing the corruption of his deeds. 
> The merit-maker rejoices. He rejoices, is jubilant, seeing the purity of his deeds. 

> The wrong-doer's tormented. He's tormented at the thought, 'I've done wrong.'  
> The merit-maker delights. He delights at the thought, 'I've made merit.'  

Wrong resolves = for sensuality, ill will, or harmfulness  
Right resolves = for freedom from sensuality, from ill will and harmlessness.  


> Follow the Dhamma 
> abandon passion, aversion, delusion; 
> be alert, the mind well released, not clinging.  


## Condensing Chapter II:  

> Heedfulness: the path to the Deathless.  
> Heedlessness: the path to death.  
> The heedful do not die.  
> The heedless are as if already dead.  

> The enlightened:  
> constantly absorbed in jhana, 
> persevering,  
> firm in their effort.  
> with initiative,  
> mindful,  
> clean in action,  
> acting with due consideration,  
> heedful, restrained.  

> Through initiative, heedfulness, restraint & self-control,  
> the wise would make an island no flood can submerge.  

> One who is wise cherishes heedfulness as his highest wealth.  

> Don't give way to heedlessness or to intimacy with sensual delight - 
> for a heedful person, absorbed in jhana, attains an abundance of ease.  

> Drive out heedlessness with heedfulness. 

> Sorrow-free he observes the sorrowing crowd.  

> Heedful among the heedless,  
> wakeful among those asleep  

> Delight in heedfulness,  
> See danger in heedlessness   


## Condensing Chapter III:  

> Hard to guard, hard to hold down, hard to see  
> The mind alights wherever it likes. 

> the sage makes it straight -  
> like a fletcher the shaft of an arrow.  

> The mind well-tamed brings ease.  
> The mind protected brings ease. 

> Those who restrain the mind  
> from Mara's bonds they'll be freed.  

> for an unsoddened mind,  
> unassaulted awareness,  
> abandoning merit & evil, wakeful,  
> there is no danger, no fear.  

> Know this body like a clay jar,  
> Secure this mind like a fort,  
> attack Mara with the spear of discernment,  
> then guard what's won  
> without settling there,  
> without laying claim.  

> Whatever an enemy might do  
> the ill-directed mind can do to you even worse.  

> Whatever a mother, father or other kinsman might do for you,  
> the well-directed mind can do for you even better.  


## Condensing Chapter IV:  

> Know this body and you go where the King of Death can’t see.

> his heart distracted:  
> death sweeps him away–  
> his heart distracted, insatiable in sensual pleasures:  
> the End-Maker holds him under his sway.  

> As a bee – without harming  
> the blossom, its color, its fragrance –  
> takes its nectar & flies away:  
> so should the sage go through a village.  


> Focus, not on the rudenesses of others,  
> not on what they’ve done or left undone,  
> but on what you have & haven’t done yourself.  

> a well-spoken word is fruitless when not carried out.  
> a well-spoken word is fruitful when well carried out.  

> No flower’s scent goes against the wind –  
> But the scent of the good does go against the wind.  
> The person of integrity wafts a scent in every direction.   

> the scent of virtue is unsurpassed.
> the scent of virtuous conduct wafts to the devas, supreme.

> Those consummate in virtue,  
> dwelling in heedfulness,  
> released through right knowing:  
> Mara can’t follow their tracks.  

> As in a pile of rubbish  
> a lotus might grow  
> so in the midst of the rubbish-like,  
> people run-of-the-mill & blind,  
> there dazzles with discernment  
> the disciple of the Rightly  
> Self-Awakened One.  


### Condensing Chapter V: Fools  

> If, in your course, you don’t meet your equal, your better, then continue your course, firmly, alone. There’s no fellowship with fools.  

> "I have wealth", the fool torments himself.
> When even he himself does not belong to himself, how then wealth?  

> A fool with a sense of his foolishness is – at least to that extent – wise.
> But a fool who thinks himself wise really deserves to be called a fool.

> Even if for a lifetime   
> the fool stays with the wise,  
> he knows nothing of the Dhamma.  

> Even if for a moment,  
> the perceptive person stays with the wise,  
> he immediately knows the Dhamma.  

--> Self-Realization does not have to take a long time.
    But it requires to be Perceptive to / prepared for the Truth.  
    And when one is not perceptive prepared for the truth, then 
    becoming perceptive/prepared might require a long time.  
    And the preparation consists in the practice of virtue. 

> Fools, their wisdom weak, are their own enemies
> as they go through life, doing evil that bears bitter fruit.

In some way, Buddha distinguishes just two kinds of people.  
The fools and the wise. Either you are are foolish or wise.  
But you may also be wise to some degree and foolish to another.  

> It’s not good,  
> the doing of the deed  
> that, once it’s done,  
> you regret,  
> whose result you reap crying,  
> your face in tears.  

> It’s good,  
> the doing of the deed  
> that, once it’s done,  
> you don’t regret,  
> whose result you reap gratified,  
> happy at heart.  

How to shorten?
> A deed done is not good to regret. 
> A deed done is good not to regret. 


On Actions, and how results develop slowly

> As long as evil has yet to ripen,  
> the fool mistakes it for honey.  
> But when that evil ripens,  
> the fool falls into pain.  

> An evil deed, when done,  
> doesn’t come out right away.  
> It follows the fool, smoldering  
> like a fire hidden in ashes.  

> Only for his ruin does renown come to the fool.  

--> Renown is for your ruin. 

> The path to material gain goes one way,  
> the way to Unbinding (Nirvana), another.  
> Realizing this, a disciple to the Awakened One,  
> shouldn’t relish offerings,   
> should cultivate seclusion instead.   



### Condensing CH VI: 

> Regard him as one who points out treasure,  
> the wise one who seeing your faults rebukes you.
> Stay with this sort of sage.  
> For the one who stays  
> with a sage of this sort,  
> things get better, not worse.  

> Let him admonish, instruct, deflect you  
> away from poor manners.  
> To the good, he’s endearing;  
> to the bad, he’s not.  

> Don’t associate with bad friends.  
> Don’t associate with the low.  
> Associate with admirable friends.  
> Associate with the best.  

--> By association with bad friends, one becomes bad.  
    By association with the moral low, one sinks down.  
    But if all would seek to associate with the good,  
    and to associate with the moral high, all would rise, 
    all would uplift; that is Sarvodaya.  
    This makes it clear, how important it is what  
    Vinoba calls the "company with the saints".  

> Drinking the Dhamma, refreshed by the Dhamma,  
> one sleeps at ease with clear awareness & calm.  
> In the Dhamma the wise one always delights. 

> Irrigators guide the water.
> Fletchers shape the arrow shaft.
> Carpenters shape the wood.
> The wise control themselves.

Here self-control is defined as what wisdom consists of. 
Or rather, 
self-control is the action of the wise. 
Self-control is what makes one wise.  
Self-control is the path to wisdom.  

> the wise are not moved by praise, by blame.  

> Everywhere, truly, those of integrity stand apart.  

> They, the good, don’t chatter in hopes of favor or gains.  

> When touched now by pleasure, now pain,  
> the wise give no sign of high or low.  

> One who wouldn’t – not for his own sake nor that of another –  
> hanker for wealth, a son, a kingdom, his own fulfillment,  
> by unrighteous means:  
> he is righteous, rich in virtue, discernment.  

--> The wise would not for his own sake nor that of another
    do anything by unrighteous means; as he is righteous.  

> Those who practice Dhamma  
> in line with the well-taught Dhamma,  
> will cross over the realm of Death  
> so hard to transcend.  

> Forsaking dark practices,  
> the wise person  
> should develop the bright,  
> having gone from home to no-home  
> in seclusion, so hard to enjoy.  

> There he should wish for delight,  
> discarding sensuality –  
> he who has nothing.  
> He should cleanse himself – wise –  
> of what defiles the mind.  

> Whose minds are well-developed  
> in the factors for self-awakening,  
> who delight in non-attachment,  
> relinquishing grasping –  
> resplendent,  
> their effluents ended:  
> they, in the world,  
> are Unbound.
