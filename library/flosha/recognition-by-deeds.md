Recognition by Deeds
Hiding of Actions
And the simplest morality that there is. 

flosha, 03.04.2026

It is said, that we recognise one by his deeds. This is true. 
But by deeds we mean not only external action, but all kinds of action. 
Within the scope of morality falls action, speech and thinking too. 

In the science of morality, as understood in the east, thinking of course is not excluded from morality. Only the fool thinks that he can have no control over his mind, because he has none. And only if one thinks that one can not have control over his mind, can one argue that thinking is no domain of morality. So its a position of a fool. Since the mind can be taken under control, one can take responsibility for his mind, for his thinking as well. 

But anothers mind cannot usually be seen; only each one can to himself see ones mind and be responsible for himself, as far as thoughts are concerned. But there are feelings too, and while thoughts can be hidden, feelings are often hard to be concealed, because we can see them in ones face, in ones eyes, in ones breath, in ones voice, in ones movement.  
Ones actions can be seen by everyone and ones speech is heard. But here too, we cannot see or hear everything. Because people do things in secret and say things in secret; which they may otherwise not do or say.  

But the actions, at least outwardly, can be restraint more easily than the speech and the speech more easily than feelings and thoughts. Therefore, by ones actions we can see ones virtue or lack of virtue in very obvious and rough ways. By ones speech, we can see it in a subtler form. By ones feelings in a yet subtler form (if they are subtle; they may as well be expressed very roughly, but then it can be seen as action or speech). 

By practicing to see all this in others, we can also learn to see it in ourselves. And by practicing to see this in ourselves, we can also learn to see it in others. 

Sometimes you will see someone, who in action is very restrained and you can find no fault about it. But in his speech you will find some impurity. This can be subtle expressions of anger, subtle expressions of jealousy, subtle expressions of desire, subtle expression of pride. You also can find such subtle impurities in the writing of people. There may be much truth in what they say or write, but still, you may be able to see that the one uttering this truth is not fully free from hindrances himself; which of course is not to be seen as a problem, as long as he isn't claiming to be free from them. 

If one can't stand someone who isn't perfect, to talk about how to become perfect, that is very stupid. It is like not being able to stand someone who talks about getting rid of addiction who struggles with an addiction. Who else would talk about this and try to solve his addiction, when not the addict? In the same way, of course will he, who talks about virtue and tries to help others with virtue, perhaps not be completely perfected in virtue; that is no requirement for speaking about it.  


## Hiding Actions

We have said before that just as people have some thoughts, that they wouldn't express and that they hide from others, as it would be bad either for the others to know or bad for oneself when others know, so there are actions that people hide from others and do in secret, either because it would be bad for others to see these actions or it would be bad for themselves, if others see these actions. 

In this respect we may evaluate and compare the ethics of the cynics with the ethics of the satyagrahi. The ethics of the cynics, to simplify, was as such, that everything that is natural is good, and whatever is natural and good is nothing to be ashamed of.  

As a consequence of this attitude, they thought, that since urinating, defacating and sexual acts with others (or as well as with oneself, since it is driven by the same natural lust) are natural, and since all that is done by dogs and other animals in public, that there can be nothing about it to be ashamed of, and therefore they have done all of that in public and did not hide it from others. They urinated without any kind of hiding, they masturbated without any kind of hiding and they had sexual intercourse with women without any kind of hiding, all in public.  

The ethics of the Satyagrahi on the other hand, can be summarised like this: What is natural is not necessarily good, since our spiritual nature is distorted; to a specific degree we must therefore rebel against what seems natural to us, while it actually isn't, as it keeps us in delusion of false identification with the body; good is only what is in accordance with our higher nature, with what we are when we are purified from everything distorting our view.
 
Based on this position, they turn the maxime around. Whatever I can only do in hiding, unless those things that everyone is doing in hiding only out of respect of others and not because he doesn't want others to *know*, shouldn't be done at all. Everyone knows that everyone urinates and it is *necessary* to urinate, so hiding the fact is pointless; if it is not done in front of each other, then not because one is ashamed for the action itself for reasons of wrong-doing, but for reasons of respect and the shame of nakedness one isn't doing it. But there are other actions, that not everyone is doing. And that aren't necessary either. Masturbation is such a case. Not everyone does it, nor is it necessary. Doing it in secret is not done for the same reason as urinating is done behind closed doors. Thus they do not say: "Do not do what you would be ashamed others *to see*", since then we had to stop even showering ourselves, when we don't want all others to see us naked. But they have put forward the maxime: "Don't do what you would be ashamed others *to know* about."   

As soon as you are involved in action that you would be ashamed others to know about, you will be ashamed of yourself as well. And as soon as you are ashamed of yourself and your action, you will have lost your integrity. As soon as you have lost your integrity, you will be dissatisfied with yourself, paralysed in action and slowly growing in depression. 

It is then directly harming yourself and others. Being ashamed of yourself without being able to overcome it, will result in shaming others. Having no integrity will spread lack of integrity around you. Being dissatisfied with yourself will result in seeking, fighting and pointing out fault in others, which will spread dissatsifaction around you and ashame *them*. You will be paralysed in action, and, rebelling against your self-inflicted paralyses, will you paralyse others. By slowly growing in depression will you pull others down.  
But there is a simple way of reversing all of it. It is to stop doing what you are ashamed of, confessing it, if not to anyone else, then to yourself or, if you wish, to God, and if you are uncapable to stop it, then to try it again and again and again, and at some point you will be able to. The most important thing is to not loose the *awareness* of being ashamed, without which you will find every possible justification to continue what you're doing. 

So this is the simple rule of the Satyagrahi, described by Narayan Desai: 
> Don't do what you would be ashamed others *to know* (and are hiding therefore).  

[quote narayan here]

And that this maxime is in accordance with the principle of truth and derived from it, is pretty clear. Because doing what one would be ashamed others to know means that one is doing what one wants to hide from others not for reasons of decency, but for reasons of untruth. Not wanting others to know the truth about something that one is doing and hiding it therefore, is simply deception; and thus a direct violation of the precept of truth. 

One may say, that this simple rule is not of much worth as a rule, since there may be others which are not ashamed of doing wrong and even want others to know what they're doing. It is possible that there may be such a person. But are you such a person?  
The Satyagrahi is not concerned with the wrong-doings of others. He is concerned with his own. And you should be concerned with your own. This is our concern.  


## Autonomy

True morality, true law is always self-given. The wise rules himself, not others. The wise need no others nor their thread of punishment to do what is right. Therefore the wise has no need for government and when one wants others to be wise as well, for wisdom to spread, then that automatically implies to free everyone else from the need for government too. 

[include quotes about the law of freedom, love etc. from the NT]

The wise also need no others doing right to do right himself. He can stick to the truth, even if the entire world is choosing untruth. What others are doing is of no concern, what we ourselves are doing is our concern. 
Therefore we apply other rules on ourselves than on others. Why? Because towards ourselves we may well be able to be strict - if trained accordingly, but towards others we may have to be very mild, if they aren't trained accordingly; initially, if we ourselves are also not trained, we might have to be very mild towards ourselves too. 

[quote vinoba here from Vichar Photi]

This leads us to a different attitude in regard to ignorance in ourselves and others. In regard to others, we should recognise, that all sin is just ignorance and we can therefore forgive them. "Forgive them, for they don't know what they do", says Jesus. But in regard to ourselves we must recognise, that ignorance itself is sin too and get rid of it wherever we find it. We should forgive ourselves too, but that doesn't imply that we haven't to train ourselves and work hard on the overcoming of ignorance, so that it can no longer rule us instead of the truth, that we want to rule our life, that we want to base our life on.
