# On Science in the teachings of Vinoba and Lanza

Vinoba coined the formula: 
> Science + Spirituality = Sarvodaya

In his view, science is necessary to bring about and is a very important part of a non-violent social order, but only if it is combined with spirituality, which in his understanding of spirituality means that science has to be guided and directed by eternal principles such as truth, non-violence and compassion. 

Lanza on the other hand had a very negative view on science and technology. But what Lanza is understanding as science and what he condemns about science is exactly science devoid of these principles.

It is due to this different view on science that the economical vision of Vinoba amounts to the idea of "village industries", in which some technology is used, but *small* technology owned by individuals or the village if necessary, while Lanzas vision amounts to a revival of medieval means of production. And it is in this way that he interpreted Gandhis approach. And he was right in doing so, but only from a particular perspective. First of all, there are many things to be learned from the medieval ways, they had many reasonable practices and organisations and some of what they did, probably much of what they did, is worth reviving without doubt.  
But that does not mean that Gandhis medieval means of production was the end goal. Gandhi and Vinoba have chosen these means of production for very specific reasons: In order to make their people and their villages independent and self-sufficient and in order to give everyone a task. That is the goal and that can be achieved by choosing these simple tools very easily, because the tools themselves can be produced locally and in this way everyone can start immediately, everyone is his own master and master of his work and when, as they have accomplished at least to a large degree, the people vow to only use the products made by themselves and their neighbours, they were able to overcome the dependence from the machines introduced by the foreign rulers by which they were able to enslave them. They say: You cannot go back in time. That is true. But certainly you can go back in regard to technology or any other thing and choose to do things as they have been done before instead. Of course you can do that, by boycott of the new ways they managed to revive the old ways. That's all. 
But that does not mean that the means must stay exactly like this for all eternity. What must stay are the underlying principles, because they indeed are of eternal validity, but the outer forms, the tools, can change.

What must stay is that the tools are owned by the workers, are as far as possible produced in the village, are owned and driven by the hands of the people as well as the fruit of their labour is owned by them. What must stay is that they do not get dependent and thereby enslaved. But if this can be achieved with modern technology and the help of science also, then this is in accordance with the philosophy.

This means, what we are doing is going back to the medieval ways of production and its tools as the *sound baseline*. Then, by sticking to the underlying principles of independence, guaranteeing the freedom and self-mastery of the craftsmen and the peasant, we can use science to improve our means of production. 

The only problem is that science and technology are now servants of money. Scientists and technologists, because they are paid by the technofeudal lords, are serving these lords and their interests and *not* the interest of the people the slightest. We have to stop this. And the only way to stop this is by radically stopping to cooperate with these lords that are abusing science and technology for their violent purposes.

This means:
* We must not use social media owned and controlled by them. If for whichever reason we are forced to use it now, then we must use it to turn against itself (e.g. by spreading awareness and information against the platforms on these very platforms).
* We must not use products owned and controlled by them. If we are forced to use them due to some kind of dependence, we can whenever possible buy *used* products in order to not support them directly. We must always buy these products which guarantee us that we can use them as long as possible and will be able to repair them. The technofeudal lords and barons do not want this, they are both against longevity of their products, which means they are against quality of their products, as well as against the possibility to repair them. This is in itself a crime against common sense morality.

Lanza rightly so asked the questions: What is non-violent education? What is non-violent economy? But he did not seem to ask: What is non-violent science? What is non-violent technology? 

In my view Lanza wasn't wrong with anything he said about science and technology. He only missed one perspective and one thought: What science can do and how science could be if guided by non-violence.

And if his followers would have been able to clarify this question, they could have avoided much of the conflicts that arose after his death over disputes of technology. Because they would have been able to use specific kinds of technology, by adhering to very specific principles in regard to it, thereby making their life - under which they had to struggle so much and that made it so hard for them to find new long-term members - easier, while at the same time keeping their precepts and acting in accordance with their rule; the rule would have been developed and interpreted in a new light very slightly and carefully instead of throwing it all over and getting rid of it.
