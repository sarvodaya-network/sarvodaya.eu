# Non-violence


Vinoba: 

> What does nonviolence mean? Love all beings to the same extent as you love your own self. (Vinoba, VP, 14.2.1959)

> Nonviolence simply does not mean 'not to commit violence'. Its one meaning is to be fearless, the second is to cooperate lovingly, and the third is to have faith in constructive work. (Vinoba, KKO, 23)  

> Nonviolence is not merely an external activity. It is an inner faith. (S, June, 1953, 721) 

> Nonviolence is the name for the maintenance of balance in thinking and not allowing the equanimity of mind to be disturbed. (S, June, 1953, 721)  

> Nonviolence is that which keeps different persons together and which tries to seek non-difference in differences, while violence is that which creates divisions and increases differences. (SY, 110)  

> A life of nonviolence means not merely occasional sacrifice but perpetual sacrifice, not merely sacrifce but joy in sacrifice. (Swaraj Shastra, 63-64) 

> In putting four lions at the top of his emblem, Ashoka has symbolically expressed the meaning of nonviolence. When bravery and love come together, there is nonviolence. (B, 13.3.1957, 5)

> Non-attachment and universal love are necessary if we wish to practice nonviolence. It has to be continuous. It is a way of life. One has to be wide awake and distinguish between selfish interest and social good. We have to free our minds from all attachments and aversions. (B, 13.6.1956, 5)  

He also once defined Satyagraha as the art of living for others. 


So, Vinoba defines nonviolence as:

* To love all beings to the same extent as you love your own self. 
* To be fearless 
* To cooperate lovingly 
* To have faith in constructive work 
* an inner faith 
* the maintenance of balance in thinking 
* not to allow the equanimity of mind to be disturbed 
* what keeps different persons together 
* what seeks non-difference in differences
* perpetual sacrifice
* joy in sacrifice
* bravery and love together
* non-attachment + universal love


So, in some way, non-violence and love are the very same.  
But there is a difference between love and non-violence to the degree to which the love is not yet pure and non-attached.

Only that love is non-violent love, which is non-different, non-attached, and thus capable to love each as oneself. To love each as oneself is the most radical thing to be done, and obviously has many revolutionary consequences, because if that is done, never would one kill another, never would one not share with another, everyone would automatically regard all possession as belonging to each, no one only to his own and no one would rule over another. Therefore, purified love automatically results in loving cooperation, which gives rise to a completely new social order devoid of economical or political conflict. 

It is thus never only a way of conduct or a particular action, but an inner faith and an attitude to others, resulting in a particular conduct. This conduct is two-fold, a negative and a positive one aspect. In the negative way it means to get rid of all violence, everything that harms the love as above described, which includes overcoming of physical violent behaviour, violent speech and violent thought, which of course also includes idea like the idea of private property and other things that are obviously in conflict with loving each as one loves oneself. In the positive way it means to put all faith in constructive work. Getting rid of all things wrong is the one thing, but true things have to be build up too. While others are working for seperation, which means doing destructive work, competing with each other, overcoming each other, stealing from each other in some kind of sanctified ways, making deals with each other; doing all these things that are in conflict with the love of others as one self, which is uniting and not separating, which is pointing to equality and not to inequality, the non-violent way is the opposite, the constructive work. Everything that is overcoming those separations and those inequalities, which are in conflict with the principle of loving each as one self, is constructive work. 

It has nothing to do with mathematical equality, with forced equality or with overcoming of natural inequalities that aren't causing any conflict; it is about righteous equality, it is about overcoming those inequalities which are against the principle of non-violence in the form of loving each as oneself. 

Also only that love is non-violent which is combined with bravery. 
True love, as the christians also say, results in fearlessness. Where there is love, there is no fear. When there is fear, there is no love. Therefore, where love is purified, there is no fear, and thus, there is bravery. 

But fearlessness can only be there through the maintenance of mental balance. What Vinoba means with balance in thinking is that our thinking is purified, that it never comes to mind that someone is any different from our selves and less to be loved; there is then nothing in the world to be feared; this is what is called the equanimity of mind; which means, that the mind cannot be disturbed. Bravery is then obviously rooted in this mental equanimity, there is no external bravery. 

Loving others as ourselves will result from reaching the equanimity of mind through purification (of our mind and our life as a whole, love being one of the aspects to be purified); when this equanimity of mind is reached, love will be purified too. We can therefore say that equanimity of mind is a requisite for complete non-violence or as well that it is non-violence itself, as no intentional violence can be committed in this equanimity.
