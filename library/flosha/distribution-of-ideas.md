## Silent Offerings of Ideas

When a servant wants to distribute ideas in written form, they should not speak to anyone unasked and annoy anyone with their message. They should only silently sit somewhere on a blanket and offer flyers or books presented in front or next to them. It will strengthen the cause if the servant wears khadi while doing so; if not yet fully clad in khadi, he can at least wear a khadi scarf or wrap himself in a khadi blanket. They may also spin with a takli or a book charkha as an additional, silent teaching. Their unobtrusive, peaceful conduct should speak for themselves and attract the right, interested people, alongside the flyers and books themselves, which we try to design in a way as to speak to the people and make them interested in the ideas offered.  

We do not want to enforce any idea, nor do we want to manipulate people with our ideas. What we want is to *offer* them our ideas, explain our ideas to them if they want to and do it in humility. Sitting on the floor, like a beggar, and not in any elevated way, is an essential aspect of this. People interested in the ideas or in what one is doing there, do not then have to come "up" to you, nor do you come to them, but people have to come *down* to you. In order to invite them to do so, one should sit on a blanket big enough for one or two others to sit next to one. This act of "coming down" to the servant is in itself a good thing. Individuals most often may not have the courage to do so, but when they are together, they will. This does not mean of course that one should not stand up. For instance, if there is an elder who starts talking to you, and who cannot sit down, one can as well stand up and talk to him in this way; it just wouldn't be the regular mode of operation.  

Thus we call this *Silent Offerings*.
