# Freedom from Systems

A Reflection on Vinobas Concept of *Tantra-Mukti*. 

by Flosha.

Vinoba called for *Tantra-Mukti*, which is usually translated as "liberation from organisation". But tantra actually is more referring to a "system". Etymologically it is related to a weft. A weft is what is interconnected with the warp into a whole. So is a system interconnected, it is a particular way of how things are in relation to others and according to particular rules. An organism is called a system too, in that it has a particular order and interconnected organs each functioning in dependence with others. Here system and organisation are one and the same.

In German we could talk of a "Körperschaft". Tantra, in a specific sense is like a body, a "Körper". In German there is also the term "Webkörper" (weave-body), which shows the relation. Vinobas *tantra-mukti* is thus not so much a call for omitting all order or all organisation (in fact he even founded some organisations himself after bringing forth this concept of *tantra-mukti*), but it is a stand against the glorification and the superiority of the body over the soul. Just as man, organisations can have a body and a soul. Some organisations may have lost or sold their soul long ago, if they even had one to start with, they are mere external, superticial structures, dead systems. Other organisations may have a soul, but their body and soul may be in conflict, because the body is not fully under control and subdued under the soul; thus the soul within will rebel and the body without will suppress the soul and hinder it. There is a third possible form of organisation, an organisation based on eternal principles that are always at the forefront, in which the soul is superior and the body is serving the soul and not vice versa.  

But this kind of organisation is only possible, this superiority of the soul over the body is only possible where the organisation is not weakening the power of the individual within. The individuals take precedence over the organisation that they constitute and therefore the organisation itself is at the background; the individual, man, is at the foreground. Where this relation turns around, the superiority of the soul is lost. In order for this to be possible, men can never be turned into numbers, nothing can ever be allowed to be done that harms their freedom, their dignity, their humanity.  
This automatically implies a certain size of the organisation that cannot be exceeded without letting the individual soul disappear and suffocate within the "mass", without sacrificing quality for quantity. This automatically implies a decentralised structure of organisation and total independence of each individual part. They may well inter-depend, but not one part solely depend on another, not many depend on a few. 

There is a difference between natural systems, natural orders of things. And unnatural, man-made systems, man-made organisations, which have lots of influence on our life, while often being completely opposed to how natural systems function.  
Our own organism, our body, is an example of a natural order. Everything about it is working harmoniously, everything is at its place and fulfills its own function for the good of all others; there is no conflict between them. Also, if one organ becomed weak, another organ tries to help. 

Vinobas *tantra-mukti* is rooted in an attitude against what man-made systems and organisations usually are. They contain unjustified hierarchies of one over another, instead of the justified and necessary hierarchy of the soul over the body, of the inner over the outer. They come with specific enforced rules. They come with a high degree of administrative demands, they are often rigid, inflexible and centralised; through centralisation they become coercive and the potential for abuse within grows. 
All these aspects could be purified by nonviolence.  

What does Sarvodaya actually want and represent? It is a non-violent social *order*. An *order* in which love, truth and compassion rule. It doesn't want a non-violent "state" or a non-violent "nation" or any other institution or organisation, nor a system, what it wants is simply an order.  

What is the difference between an order and an organisation or system?  
An order is a state, while organisation is the process of bringing that state about.  
When entering my room, one will find a particular order of things. It simply means that every thing has a particular place. It has the place that I have determined for it. I can change this order at any time, because I am the one who is ordering my things. The process of doing that, this may be called "organisation". 

But in so-called "organisations" the situation has been turned around. No more the people are ordering things, but the order of things is ordering people. It is not always about things, so we may say situations.  
Instead of people ordering situations, which changes situations, situations order people, which changes people.  
A particular order is prescribed and people are ordered accordingly. That is what Vinoba means with "tantra" and that is what he wants us to be free from.  

We can have order without an organisation or a system, as long as people order situations and situations do not order people.  

That is also the meaning behind the term *anarchy*. Anarchy means order without coercion, order without external authority, order not-imposed from a ruler above. God is no ruler from above, God is a ruler from within and beyond; therefore in Sarvodaya the term *anarchy* is equated with the rule of God and the rule of God is equated with anarchy; both are equated with *swaraj* (self-rule), and *swaraj* is equated with *gram-swaraj* (village self-rule), as that is how the self-rule of each can find practical expression in a social order, through complete autonomy of the communities consisting of the individuals in control of their affairs, ordering their situations themselves. Because the autonomy and autarky of local communities is the only possible form of organisation in which the individual is not subdued under a system, in which every individual can order the situation instead of being ordered by the situation, in which the soul can reign over the body in the socio-economic field.

How does such an order of things work and how can it be brought about? It has to do with the outherness vs. the innerness of order.  
Order can either come from outside, being enforced down unto someone in a downward motion; then it is oppressive and enslaving. Or it can arise from the inside, in an upward motion; then it is non-oppressive and liberating. 

Now, that doesn't mean that there may not be orders that are more truthful, more nonviolent, that are to be preferred, as we proclaim *gram-swaraj* to be. But the point is that such an order can only ever be *offered*. It can not be enforced. 

Since a non-violent order is by definition a non-coercive order, it is not just "wrong" to enforce a non-violent order, it is *impossible* to do so.  
Therefore a non-violent order can only ever be *offered*. We can offer ideas, we can give advise, but we must give total freedom to accept these ideas or not, to follow the advise or not. Only if the ideas or advise are deliberately accepted, as one is convinced that they are right, will one be ordered accordingly from within (one is ordering himself) and can an outer order arise from within. 

Man has an inner order and is surrounded by an outer order. The more the inner order is opposed to the outer order, the more the inner and the outer order will be in conflict. This will cause unrest within and without. Internal and external violence, mental and social conflict will be the result.   
Men will always strive to order the world in a way that is in accordance with their inner order. At the same time, the inner order of men will always be influenced and distorted by the outer order, as long as they aren't so deeply grounded within themselves that no outer order can influence any control over their inner order; that nothing can bring them out of their balance within. 

If men reach this deeply founded order within, so that nothing can shake them, also called the *equanimity of mind* or the *steadfast wisdom* or the *right view*, then nothing will be able stop their inner order to emanate into a nonviolent social order without. 

But if they do not reach this inner order, then nothing that they outwardly do will be capable to ensure any true betterment of order nor any freedom from coercion, as they will keep living under orders coming from without, feeling pressured and enforced by outer orders, living *under* orders, instead of having an order under them; as they truly are meant to be above order, the source of order, since no order can be without them ordering it. 


## Tantra-Mukti in Practice

How then can *tantra-mukti* be practiced? 
* **Order the preferred form of non-violent organisation:** In the nonviolent context, an "Order" is preferred over "organisation", "system" or "institution", as it is a form of sanctified organisation, it implies the organisation being based on deeper principles, which in regard to any non-violent order, at the bare "minimum", are non-violence and truth. Nonviolent organisation therefore means organising in the tradition of e.g. medieval orders of the past, and we may call these orders non-systematic or *tantra-mukti* orders. 
* **The only nonviolent Union possible:** An order can also be called a union, but it cannot be a union *against* any other. The Sarvodaya Union is therefore by definition the union of unions. A non-violent order can be a brotherhood, a sisterhood, a family, a village, a community, an individual, it can be whatever, being based on the principles, is able to exist without opposing others.  
* **Necessary informality:** There is no so called official "membership" within the order; it is not only always voluntary, but also always informal. One does not belong to a nonviolent order because a paper says so, but because he himself says so, by proclaiming agreement to the order and adherence to its principles, e.g. by means of vow. Membership is expressed solely by one living accordingly and members recognize each other *by their deeds*. One orders ones life in harmony with the order, in that one makes that order ones own. One is never owned by an order, one is "applying" and *expressing* order. That does not exclude necessary minimal formalities, but it never allows formality to reign, as there is no formality to truth. 
* **The place of Precepts and a Rule in an Order:** Just as anarchy is in no way opposed to order, but it is order without coercive authority, so is anarchy in no way opposed to rules or "law", but against enforced rules and violent law; it is for deliberately accepted rules and nonviolent law. One who does not accept any order imposed on him, one who cannot be forced by rule or law to obey, does not imply that he has no order, wants no order, follows no rule and obeys no law. He has an inner order, wants the outer order to reflect this inner order, follows rules he has given to himself and obeys the law that asks for obedience, never enforces the same, as non-coercion is part of the law. In fact he may follow stricter rules than any one under order; nonviolence is such a strict rule. Thus, a non-violent order can have a rule, that those who give the order their consent, can freely accept and then deliberately adhere to. But most of all it must have vows or precepts, and these precepts stand above the rule, they are the heart of the rule, they are the eternal and timeless foundation on which the rule has to evolve organically. It is due to them that the rule can never abuse the individual and the individual can not abuse the rule; for when e.g. the rule requires one to obey then this rule of obedience is based on the principles of nonviolence and truth; if thus the obedience demanded e.g. by a general of the Peace Army is in opposition to nonviolence or opposed to truth, then obviously the order given is not in accordance with the precepts and is therefore no expression of the rule; disobedience to such an order is then right application of the rule inspite of the rule demanding obedience, as it demands obedience only on the basis of the precepts. The precepts are the soul of an order; loosing this soul does not imply a change of the order, but death and destruction of the order; but since the soul is immortal, the order is so too; so any order founded based on such principles is immortal by definition too; as whatever is outwardly done to it in opposition to its principles is never destroying the order that exists beyond external form, as a path offered and always possible to be chosen; it only implies individuals diverting from the path.
* **Precedence of the Precepts over Rules:** Whenever time and need should require a change of rules, they can be modified, as long as they are still clearly adhering to the precepts; the rule should thus not be changed in the sense of getting more lax, but on the contrary, the rule, just as the individuals, should underwent a process of constant purification. If a rule change seems necessary, then because it has been too time-bound, too limited before, because it has been based on some kind of limited understanding of truth; so any change of the rule should improve the rule in the light of a more trueful understanding of the underlying principles. But these principles and the commitment to these principles must never be given up.
