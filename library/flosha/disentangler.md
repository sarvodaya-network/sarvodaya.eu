It is not easy to write such a book for children, because you can only write for children if you know how children think.

Writing for children means to write as such, that *nothing* is assumed to be already known. That means that I will explain everything anew. This makes it easy to understand.  

Many writer write for adults. And then, they write for very specific adults. For such adults who already know very much or think in their own ways. Their books are very hard to understand and lead to many misunderstandings; most people will not read them at all, even adult people, because they are too confusing. Because, you know: No one knows everything. Everyone only knows a little bit. 

If you already have to know a lot to understand something else, it remains hard to understand. And you know it well: If something is very hard and no one helps you and guides you, then you are too frustrated and you give up. This is the same for adults too!  

Maybe sometimes you think that adults know everything better than you, right? But this is not true! For sure, they know some things better than you. Then it is good to learn from them, so that you can know them too. But it is very well possible, that you know some things better than an adult. Maybe an adult doesn't know something that you know! Adults can also learn from a child. Believe me if I tell you, that I learned many things from you.

## (3) About Understanding and Misunderstanding

Some things in life are hard to understand. Therefore it is better to try to understand them as early as you can, because then you have more time to learn to understand them!  

And you know what? There are even things that are much harder to understand when you are older, and easier to understand, when you are still a child, because often when someone gets older, he will already have a lot of understanding, but some of his understanding will be *misunderstanding*! 

Misunderstanding is when you think that you understand, but your understanding is not right. So Misunderstanding is the reason for very much confusion! 

And when you have a misunderstanding for a long time, then it is hard to get rid of it. Imagine: When you think for many years that something is right, then it is hard to accept it, if someone tells you, that it is wrong! Some people then will never accept it. They rather live with something, of which they know deep down that something is wrong with it, then to admit that they were wrong... that is very sad.  


### Why is there misunderstanding?

I am sure that you misunderstood some things before.  
But why is that so?  

Very often it is because of this: *There is a misunderstanding because someone has not explained it very well.*  

This has happened to you before, right?  
Someone told you something, but you did not understand. He didn't explain it very well to you.  
Can you remember such a situation? What happened next?  

Sometimes this is because the people that you ask the question simply cannot explain it any better. It is difficult to explain something and not everyone can explain everything well. But sometimes they *could* explain it better, but they do not do so, because they *think* that you already understood, but you haven't.  

Sometimes they ask: "Did you understand?"  
Often children nod their head, to say: "Yes, yes, I understood", but actually... they are still confused. But why do they say that they understand if they don't? Why do they lie?  
Sometimes they do so, because they are ashamed.  
They think: "He has explained it to me. I didn't understand it... But I don't want him to think that I am stupid. So I am too ashamed to admit it."  
That means: You lie because of shame. Or you keep silent because of shame. I can understand this very well. But it would be better if you would not be ashamed and wouldn't lie, right?  

That means: *Sometimes, there is misunderstanding because of shame, because one doesn't want to admit to not understand.*
And: *Sometimes there is misunderstanding because of lying, because one is doing as if he understands while he doesn't understand.*

But why is that, that you feel ashamed for not knowing something? Should you feel ashamed for not knowing or not understanding something? What do you think?  
I think no! Not knowing something or not understanding something is never a reason to be ashamed.  
But why then do you feel so? Maybe you feel so, because sometimes people have given you this feeling. You wanted to know something, but they were very annoyed and they have given you the feeling that you are stupid and therefore you are ashamed now, because you don't want that they think you are stupid, because that is not fair, right?  

How should you know if no one has told you? How should you understand if no one explained it to you?  
There is only one thing that we should be ashamed of: Not to ask questions.  
We always should ask questions. If you never raise a question, you may never know the answer to the question.  
It is okay to not know. But if it is something important, you should *want* to know.  

But you should know this: Sometimes people do not want that you ask questions, that is why they give you these bad feelings if you do. Because some questions hurt them. That doesn't mean that these questions are bad. It rather means that there is something bad about themselves and they don't want you to question it, because it hurts if something bad is revealed.  

Imagine that there is a thief. He secretly tries to steal your lollipop from behind your back. Of course he doesn't want you to know, so he is very careful, he is sneaking behind your back, hiding in the shadow, while you look in the other direction. But imagine that somehow, in the corner of your eye, you catch the thief and you think: "What? There is a thief! Does he try to steal my lollypop!?"  
Imagine now, that you would turn around and look him directly into the eyes. The thief would be shocked and think by himself: "Oh no, she has seen me!" Then you ask him this question:  
"Mr. Thief, are you going to steal my lollipop?!"  
What do you think the thief would say to you? Is he happy that you have seen him and that you ask him this question?  
Would he say: "Yes! I will steal it from you!" Do you think the thief would be so honest? He wanted to steal it secretly. He didn't want you to know it. Why does he want to hide it? Because he knows very well that it is your lollipop. He knows that he shouldn't steal it. So when you ask him, he most likely will not be honest to you, because he is ashamed. Maybe he will then say: "Oh no! Never would I steal your lollipop! I was just going to look at it!" He will lie, because he is ashamed and doesn't want to admit it.    
That means, *people who do something, for which they are secretly ashamed, because there is something wrong about it, do not like it when someone asks questions about it and are unlikely to answer honestly*. 

In other cases, people do not ask questions at all or they do not ask the right questions and that is causing misunderstandings. What could that be?  

For example, there might be a misunderstanding about what someone feels or what he does or what he thinks, why he feels what he feels or why he does something that he does or why he thinks what he thinks. 

For example, imagine this:  
Everyday you see a man. The man walks past your house and along the street. Then he turns around and goes the other direction. The next day he comes again, this time he goes a little bit further, but suddenly he stops and goes back again. Everyday you see him walking like this. Everyday he goes a little bit further, but always he suddenly stops, and goes back.  
This is what you see. But why is he doing that? Can you know why? You cannot know.  
* You ask your father: "Father, why is this man walking like that?"  
Your father may say: "Maybe he is afraid of something! He wants to go somewhere, but he doesn't have enough courage. Everyday he goes a few steps further, in order to overcome his fear! And someday he will reach his goal and will be no longer afraid!"  
* Then you ask your mother: "Mother, why is this man walking like that?" Your mother may say something very different: "Maybe this man had a surgery on his leg. He has to be very careful. Everyday he goes a few steps further, in order to build up the strength in his weak leg. He does it so very carefully in order not to hurt his leg and build the strength back up very slowly! And someday his leg will be fully healed and he will no longer have pain!"  
* Then you ask your brother: "Brother, why is this man walking like that?" And your brother may say: "Maybe this man has a mental disease. He's crazy. Everyday he wants to do something. When he starts walking from his home he knows what he wants to do. But at some point he forgets what it was and then he turns around and goes home. Everyday he tries to remember and he keeps saying it to himself in order to not forget. He makes progress and can remember it for longer! Maybe someday he remembers it until he reaches his destination? Who knows..."  
All this would be possible. Who is right? Who is wrong? You cannot know this. You can only keep thinking about it. Or you can ask the man. *Only if you ask the right question to the right person will you be able to get the answer*. 

Imagine you go outside and ask him the next day. "Why are you walking like that? Is it because of this or because of that?" And he may say, that: "No, I am not afraid of something. No, I didn't have a surgery on my leg. No, I do not forget what I wanted to do." 
It may turn out that it is entirely different than everything that your father or your mother or your brother or yourself have imagined. 

"Everyday I walk this path because of my wife. I used to walk this path with her. We used to walk to the park together. Last year she died. I was so sad that I couldn't go to the park anymore, because it always remembers me of these moments with her that I can no longer have, because she is no more here. But someday I decided that I have to go there again, to overcome this sadness. When I first tried, I broke out in tears after half of the way and went back home. Everyday I made it a bit further before the sadness was too strong for me to endure. Then I went back home. Someday I will be able to reach the park and relieve myself from this sadness."

You see. Another misunderstanding. 
You may think this man is injured or forgetful or afraid, that he wants to overcome his injury or his forgetfulness or his fear; and it turned out that it was nothing like that. He was neither injured nor forgetful nor afraid. He was actually sad, trying to overcome his sadness. Such misunderstandings happen everyday and all over the world and in everyones life. We think that something is somehow, and in reality it is not so. But as long as we do not ask the question, the right question, to the right person, we do not know how it really is and we may think the wrong thing.  

So: *Sometimes there is a misunderstanding because no questions are raised or not the right questions are raised or they are raised to the wrong persons*.

Asking your father doesn't help you in understanding the reasons of another man. Asking your mother doesn't help you either. It may be a very good first step to ask them. But your father and your mother cannot know it either. They can only guess. And they should tell you so. They should tell you: "I really don't know why! Maybe it is this, or it is that, but it also could be very different!"

There is another example.   
Maybe you see two people and they have a dispute. Maybe your father shouts at your mother and you don't like this. Now you could ask this question: "Father, why do you shout so loud at my mother?!"  
Because if he would answer this question, you could *understand* better, why that is happening.  
But maybe you are afraid and don't want to ask questions, if he is so angry, although it would be a very good question. But if the father is very angry he will not like this question. Maybe it will even make him more angry, you know? But that doesn't mean that the question is wrong, it is a very good question.  
If he would answer the question honestly, then maybe he would say something like this: "I am shouting so loud, because I am very angry about your mother." And then you could ask: "But why are you so angry about her?" And so on. If you would keep asking questions like this, and when your father would keep answering them honestly, then maybe, in the end, it would turn out that it is very unnecessary of him to be so angry and that it would be much better to be calm and lovely to each other. And you would like that too, isn't it?  

But the thing is this: When your father is so angry, he will be too angry to be honest. He cannot be honest then. And you will be too afraid to raise these questions. You will have these questions in your head, but you will not be able to get them out of your mouth. 

So, *this is another reason for misunderstanding: Fear.* Maybe you could understand him, but you are too afraid to ask.  

You will only be able to think: "But why he is so angry? Why can he not be nice now?" But what do you think is your father thinking in this moment, when he is so angry? Nothing. He cannot think then.

  because one is not able to do so mentally/verbally / can not explain it

* Because it is not well enough explained since the other doesn't ask 
  (he doesn't explain it as not seeing a need to)
  * because one doesn't want to know the truth (it could hurt)
  out of being ashamed to ask further or ask at all 
  out of being afraid to ask due to consequences (e.g. the other one is angry)
  out of being too proud to ask 

* Or it isn't explained because one doesn't want to explain it
  * because one wants to hide what one knows 
     * as revealing it could hurt (you or him) and he wants to protect himself or you
     * or for his own advantage because it would put him into a worse position when you know it too. 
  * because of pride (my knowledge, not yours)
  * because of shame: I could explain it, but I am ashamed to do so.
  * because of fear... 

In any way, it boils down to:
*There are misunderstandings about things or about others because one doesn't explain a thing or himself at all or not well enough, as one cannot do so or cannot do it well enough, or as one doesn't want to do so or not enough or because one doesn't know that explanation or more explanation is needed as it isn't asked for.*

### Misunderstanding oneself

But not only things or others and their feelings can cause misunderstandings. We also can have misunderstandings about ourselves. 

What do you think? Do you understand yourself?  

I am sure, that you think: "If I do not understand myself, who then?" Right. No one is closer to you than yourself. Only you know what is going on in your head and in your heart. 
You have thoughts that you tell no one about. Sometimes you have feelings that you tell no one about. Me too. Everyone has such.  
So there are secrets in you and only you know them. And there are secrets in others that only they know.  
But what if there are also some secrets in you, that you yourself do not know about? And things in you, that you do not yet understand?  

There are misunderstandings about ourselves.  
Sometimes I do not know why I do what I do.
Sometimes I do not know why I think what I think. 
Sometimes I do not know why I feel what I feel.
Isn't that so?  
Sometimes I may not even know *that* I think, *that* I feel, *that* I do. I just think, feel and do, but while I think, feel and do, I am not aware of thinking, feeling and doing. Can you imagine?  

Imagine you sit down and watch your friends playing. You start thinking: "What do they do know? What are they playing? Do they want me to play with them? Should I go there?" You just think something. When now a friend comes to you and asks you: "Hey, what are you doing here?", if you were honest, maybe you could reply: "Oh, I was just thinking." But it also could well be, that you say: "Nothing! Lets play!" Why is that? Why do you say nothing, when you were thinking? Do you think that thinking is nothing? Or maybe your thinking was hiding from yourself so good, that you didn't notice that you were thinking and you have forgotten it immediately when it happened, can that be? 

The point is this: Most often, when you think, you do not do this yourself. You do not even want this yourself. It just happens. Thinking is like an animal. It does what it wants. If it wants to go right, it goes right. If it wants to go left, it goes left. It happens within you, you let it happen, you witness it happening. As if you watch a movie in your head.  
And this movie never stops. It is always running. Sometimes you do not notice it much, sometimes you notice it very much. But there is always something going on.  
Constantly thinking says something like:  
"How could it be?"
"How was it?"  
"How will it be?"  
"How has it been?"  
"Why is it over?"  
"What now?"  
"What to do?"  
"Why this?"  
"What is happening?"  
"Why does he does this?"  
All these questions are going on inside and the mind replies itself. It offers solutions, it offers constant possibilities and constant explanations. The mind is working very hard and it wants to help you, but it isn't very good at it yet.  
It causes confusion. It often doesn't help you much, because it is driven by very conflicting forces.

Imagine there is a dog and he has two leaches around his neck. One leach pulls him into one direction and then another one pulls him into another direction. This dog will always be torn apart, sometimes going left, sometimes going right; he doesn't know what is right, where to go, and sometimes it pulls stronger to one side and sometimes it pulls stronger to the other side.  

That is how it is with your mind. But what are the leaches?  
It is your *values*. 
What you value, that your mind will try to go to, try to strengthen, try to collect for you, try to be.  

Since you value conflicting things, there is conflict in your mind.

You didn't know yet that *you* can move them. You neither knew that you have them, nor how to use them and you had to learn all this at first. Then you learned to walk and at first this was very hard and difficult. And now it is very easy.  

What if there is a world just like this, but within, in yourself? What if there is something in yourself, that is like your body, where you do not even know that it is there and that you can control it? Just as you didn't know yet when you were born that you can move your body, maybe you do not know yet now, that you can move your mind and even if you know that it is possible to move it yourself, you may not yet be able to do it or to do it well, because it is difficult to learn and it has to be trained, just like walking or any other movement of your body.

## The Overwhelming Feeling

And isn't it, that he could also ask himself this same question. He could ask: "Why am I so angry now? What is going on?" But do you think that he asks himself this question, in this moment, when he is so angry? 

No. He is not able to do so. Why is that?  

It is because this angry feeling is *confusing* him. He is too confused to think right.  
You may know this from yourself. If you are very angry, or if you are very sad or have any other very strong feeling, then you often cannot see anything else anymore. You only see this anger or this sadness or another feeling. And in this moment, you often do not understand yourself. The feeling creates so much confusion, it is as if it is eating you up.  

Maybe you had a cat and then it died. When this happened, you were very sad and all you could feel was this sadness. Maybe someone said something very mean to you and this made you very angry. When this happened, you were so angry that all you could feel was this anger. Maybe someone did something that ashamed you and in that moment all you could feel was this shame. Maybe someone threatened you and in that moment all you could feel was fear. Maybe once something hurt in your body and then all you could feel was that pain.  

You see? Sometimes, if the feelings are very strong, they make it very hard for you to do anything else than being in this feeling. It is as if this feeling is holding you firmly. It controls you. But whenever something controls *you*, that means that you have lost the control. 

Maybe you were told that this is normal. That everyone experiences this. This is true. And for many people this will be so for their entire life. 
But the wise say, that this is not necessarily so. They say that there is a way to stop this from happening. And they have training methods to achieve this. But only very few people are ready to do this training. Some people don't know about it. Some people don't believe that it is possible, but they didn't even try. 









When the feeling is very strong, the thinking is very weak. 

Have you ever experienced this, that you did something and later you thought: "Oh no, why have I done this? What was I thinking? How stupid!" Maybe you did this, because at that moment you were thinking nothing. You couldn't think. Only now you thought about it and then you realised that it was not good.

What does that mean? It means that is is always better to think before you do! But if you cannot think, because the feelings are too strong, what can you do then?  

The question is: Why are these feelings there at all? And why are they so strong? Why do we feel fear, anger or sadness? Why do we feel shame? Why is that?  











You do not understand him, because he is so angry, you do not understand why he is angry. 




That means: Sometimes, people do not only misunderstand each other, they also misunderstand themselves!  

 







 Instesd you also could say this: "No! I did not understand this yet, what does that mean? Can you explain it a bit more to me?" 
And this would be very good, because then there would be less confusion.
