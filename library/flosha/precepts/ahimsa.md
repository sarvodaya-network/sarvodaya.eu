# Ahimsa

(2) **Ahimsa** is a direct consequence of truthfulness, it is truthfulness in mutual relations. I also defined it as "truth in action". Any action, that is not nonviolent, reveals itself as being opposed to truth in some way or another.  

How to think of others, how to speak of others, how to act upon others is all contained within non-violence. Lying and deceiving is violence, and hurting others is in itself deceiving others and oneself. Not to consciously, deliberately kill is the most outer aspect (as in the abrahamitic command: You shall not kill), in a more refined way it means more generally not to consciously, deliberately do *any harm* or hurting any other sentient being, which goes way beyond the more rough demand of the law of Moses. This is the negative aspect of Ahimsa. 

Love devoid of violence, without unmerciful differentiation, which in christian terms is called Barmherzigkeit, in buddhist terms is called Metta, is the positive aspect of ahimsa.  
A vegetarian diet is just one of the many consequences of this idea. Non-violent communication is another one. Anarchy is another one. If one realises the inherent violence of the state, the mechanised embodiment of violence, it is a logical conclusion to strive for a traditional, stateless society, as the followers of Gandhi are doing.  

Just as Ahimsa is deduced from Satya and Satya can be deduced from Ahimsa, and every other virtue or principle is logically derived from these two.
