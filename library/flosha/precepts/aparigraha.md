### Aparigraha, Non-Possession or voluntary Poverty

> Nothing is enough for him, for whom enough is too less. (Epikur)

Aparigraha means following a simple rule: 
Don't hold on to what you don't need. 
Have what you need. Don't have what you don't need. 
Do not consider you as a proprietor of anything, but as a custodian. 
Reduce your needs. Ask yourself what you really need, reduce your needs to a reasonable base-line and get rid of what you don't need. 

Voluntary poverty is not as much about getting rid of things and consciously verzichten on something, but it is primarly about recognising that you *are* poor already and necessarily so, that you *can* not possess anything in this world and that it is not your responsibility to possess either. Neither to possess nor to be possessed. It is impossible to possess anything. You just have it around you, you use it. There may be land that you toil. And it is common sense and a matter of non-violence that we aren't taking away from someone what he actively uses, as that means to steal. But if someone claims to possess land and this land lies around, isn't touched by him and is cultivated by others, this is not just an unfortunate way of affair, but it is simply *not true*. 

> Land belongs to those who toil it. - Tolstoi
