# Brahmacharya

(3) **Brahmacharya** is sense restraint in its negative aspect, true love beyond form in its positive aspect. In its less strict application it is refraining from sexual misconduct, which means celibacy in the christian and muslim sense (sex within marriage, no masturbation, no sexual intercourse without accepting the possible consequence of procreation, no sex without love, no sexual abuse, including mutual abuse of each other etc.) Because "true love can not exist where there is ... of the body." (quote from Vinoba in the talks on the gita somewhere). If one realises the untruthfulness and violence in sexual affairs and in the craving for ones body, more strict celibacy (abstaining from all sexual activity in body, mind or speech) and sense restraint is a logical conclusion. Sense restraint is meant here in the sense in which it is teached by the Buddha as well as in the Bhagavad-Gita in India as well as, although perhaps less technically, in other traditions, such as by Meister Eckhart in medieval Germany. 

* Abstinence from any kinds of intoxicants is another necessary aspect of this vow, for which the Buddhists have an extra precept, but it is included in Brahmacharya. Abstaining from intoxicants is a logical conslusion from Satya, as anything by which you consciously rob yourself from the ability of sane judgement is impeding your ability to be truthful. It is as well a logical conclusion from Ahimsa, as intoxicating ones body means consciously harming ones body and by diminishing ones consciousness one diminishes ones self-control and increases the risk to hurt others both in emotional and physical ways significantly.
