# Brahmacharya

(3) **Brahmacharya** 

Brahmacharya is sense restraint in its negative aspect, true love beyond form in its positive aspect.

In its less strict application it is refraining from sexual misconduct, which means celibacy in the christian and muslim sense (which in short means sex within marriage, no masturbation, no sexual intercourse without accepting the possible consequence of procreation, no sex without love, no sexual abuse, including mutual abuse of each other etc.) Because "true love can not exist where there is ... of the body." (quote from Vinoba in the talks on the gita somewhere). 

If one realises the untruthfulness and violence in sexual affairs and in the craving for ones body, more strict celibacy (abstaining from all sexual activity in body, mind or speech) and sense restraint is a logical conclusion. Sense restraint is meant here in the sense in which it is teached by the Buddha as well as in the Bhagavad-Gita in India as well as, although perhaps less technically, in other traditions, such as by Meister Eckhart in medieval Germany. 

Vinoba claimed that:  
> As long as Brahmacharya is not observed, the observance of the vows of truth and nonviolnce is well-nigh impossible.  

So as the other vows, Brahmacharya is a natural consequence of truth and nonviolence. But why is that so? Does it mean that one can only be fully truthful and nonviolent, if one is completely celibate?  

The Buddha would say yes to this question. But the Christians would say that celibacy exists in Marriage as well, in another form. It is a matter of debate whether this kind of celibacy, as practiced in marriage, is "incomplete" compared to the other and whether its only better to a degree from sexual misconduct, while only complete abstinence is pure. This is the ascetic position. But ere it is condemned, we should understand where this idea is coming from. 

If one is truly seeing every single being as identical with himself, if any kind of differentiation is removed. If one does not feel like the owner of ones owm body and senses, as one is fully liberated from this sort of attachment to the body, then there simply is no sexual desire left. Therefore they deduce that when any kind of sexual action is still there, sexual desire is still there and when sexual desire is still there, there can be no freedom from the wrong view and thus no complete truth and nonviolence.  

This assumption rests on a premise. If sexual action is impossible without unwholesome desire, then any sexual action would be against truth and nonviolence. If sexual action is possible without unwholesome desire, then sexual action would not necessarily be against truth and nonviolence. Whether this is the case or not, I don't know.  

But I know that many saints loved in such a way that it was utterly impossible for them to love an individual in such a way that there could arise any kind of wish to become sexual with one another.   

---

Abstinence from any kinds of intoxicants is another necessary aspect of this vow, for which the Buddhists have an extra precept, but it is included in Brahmacharya. Abstaining from intoxicants is a logical conslusion from Satya, as anything by which you consciously rob yourself from the ability of sane judgement is impeding your ability to be truthful. It is as well a logical conclusion from Ahimsa, as intoxicating ones body means consciously harming ones body and by diminishing ones consciousness one diminishes ones self-control and increases the risk to hurt others both in emotional and physical ways significantly.
