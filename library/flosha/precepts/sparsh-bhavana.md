# Sparsh-Bhavana

The vow originally was Gandhijis "Removal of Untouchability". And on first sight, this seems to be a very particular Indian phenomenon, as only there are the so-called Untouchables, outcastes.   
In order to give this vow a more universal and more positive meaning, Vinoba changed it to Sparsh-Bhavana, the feeling of touchability.  
The meaning is this: We shouldn't feel that anyone isn't worthy to be touched (in the metaphorical sense, not necessarily in a literal sense), we should feel that we can get in touch with everyone. 

When we interpret untouchability in this universal sense, we will see, that it is not a mere indian phenomenon at all.   
We see everywhere in the world today that some people are not willing to connect with certain other people in any way. They despise to talk to them, yes they even see it as their duty not to do that. As is currently the case with Russia. They stop diplomacy, and when some diplomat suggests that we should of course get in touch with Russia and try to talk with them in order to establish peace, they stand up in protest.  

Whoever we declare to be the enemy becomes an untouchable to us. He is then no longer a real human being like others and that makes it possible to commit all the violence against one another.  

The vow of sparsha-bhavana means, that we must have no enemies. It doesn't matter if someone considers us their enemy – we do *not* consider him an enemy of us.  

We can therefore also call it Nicht-Feindseligkeit. Or, in its positive sense: Universal Friendship, universal brotherhood with all men, none excluded.  

Thus it is of course included in non-violence, but it is particularly mentioned due to its importance.
