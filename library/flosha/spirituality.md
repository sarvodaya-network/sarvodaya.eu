


I will argue that there is not only a common ground, but a common art of spirituality with shared techniques, which while they appear different on the surface, are done to the same end. And if we understand these techniques and how the *practice* actually works, independent of the outer form, we can then practice the outer form that suits our disposition, without being in delusion about the effectiveness or ineffectiveness of other forms of practice, because we understand the essence of what makes a practice work and what doesn't. 

When in Christianity the monks pray X times a day, when in Islam the Muslim prays five times a day as the recommended minimum, this is a method of establishing a different base line, a different orientation, to bring about the religion in the actual sense of the term.  
Aguéli describes it as an "insulation". It is this religious practice of prayer that insulates against the world and turns one towards the eternal, to the Absolute. And the best prayer, according to Muhammed, is the silent one. But what is silent prayer? It may be a silent dialogue with god, it may as well be a self-inquiry in the buddhist sense of the term, or meditation as non-doing in the early meaning. When Buddha now advises the monks to dwell in solitude and to stop distracting themselves, to meditate, then that is as well "insulating" them from the world.  
Aguéli turns this around and calls it a "distraction from the world". That is not opposed to his view, as it is always distraction through the world, distraction with the world, that the buddhist teaching opposes. With distraction from the world Aguéli surely doesn't mean to distract oneself with sacred stuff in order to forget about the profane stuff, it just means, by and through the practice, being pulled towards the Divine (or out of ones conditioning) and thus in certain ways away from the world in the sense of ones attachments to it. 

This technique have all in common. The outer form is different, but they all try and suggest a practice to everyone that is meant to help them to become *free*. Yes, they want you to be disciplined in this way; but the discipline has to be taken up voluntarily and is only meant to ultimately free yourself. Just as warm clothing insulates one against cold, the spiritual practice, done throughout the day, insulates one against worldly afflictions and allows one to stay detached; and the more so the more the practice is purified and purifies the practitioner.
