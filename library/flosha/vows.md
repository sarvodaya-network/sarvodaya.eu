# Vows

> Bapu (Gandhiji) taught us that there are some values of life which are eternal and unchangeable. One should have complete faith in them.  
> Among them are on the one hand nonviolence, non-possession, truth, etc., and on the other, Swadeshi and the removal of untouchability have been added to them. This leaves an impression that their texture is not uniform. Nonviolence, truth, etc., are basic observances, while Swadeshi and removal of untouchability are secondary ones derived from them, and equality of all religions, bread labour and such others are comparatively of less importance than truth, non-violence, etc. Thus, they are a mixture of both primary and secondary items. They are not in consonance with the thinking of those persons who think in the manner of Shastras. Yet, they embody a good deal of the essence of spirituality. (AP, 33-34)

By this formulation, "not in consonance with the thinking of those persons who think in the manner of Shastras", I am sure, did also apply to himself to some degree. What he is writing here is that the consistency of the vows as Gandhiji had established them is not optimal in terms of the logical structure underlying them. That is not a critique about any of their *content*, and thus it also never came to mind to him to get rid of any aspect of them and to remove anything; yet we can say that it might be possible to present these same aspects in a more coherent, more consistent manner; if this helps to express the truth and to promote nonviolence better, then Gandhiji and Vinobaji would fully agree to such a change, which is only a change in form, a purification in form and maybe an addition of lacking aspects, but in no way does it imply to loose anything they offer.  

> The number of the observances can be less ore more and it does vary, but nonviolence, truth and some others are eternal. (VP no. 422 of 1957)

> They (the vows) have the capacity of protecting men and society. As a result of their observance, an individual like Bapu (Gandhiji) became a Mahatma. *They embody the quintessence of the lives of all saints*. They are not only meant for a recluse. They are for him and for the whole society. (VP, 17.2.1959)  

> It is said these days that 'vows' constitute a bondage, but it is a misleading idea.  
> The vows do not give trouble. ... They form a good straight path. When the road is good, we do not worry. Vows imply treading a straight path. They make it easy, they make men free. (MA, 28)
