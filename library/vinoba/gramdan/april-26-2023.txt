Recently, a village in Maharashtra moved to the Bombay High Court, demanding the implementation of the Gramdan Act, which gives wide powers and responsibilities to the gram-mandal (Gram Sabha) for the administration, development, and welfare of the village. The high court bench had issued notices and sought replies from the principal secretaries of the forest and rural development departments, the divisional commissioner, and the district collector, all of whom have been made respondents in the case, and a hearing is awaited.

The Bhoodan movement, often known as the bloodless revolution, was an Indian voluntary land reform campaign started by Gandhian Vinoba Bhave. It was philosophically influenced by the Sarvodaya movement, and Congress backed the movement. JP Narayan also left active politics in 1953 to join the Bhoodan movement. The Bhoodan movement was aimed at persuading wealthy landowners to give up a portion of their property voluntarily to landless people. Landless labourers were given tiny parcels of land on which to dwell and raise their crops. The movement later evolved into the Gramdan movement, in which the entire or a significant portion of a village's land was donated by at least 75% of the residents for equal distribution among all village residents.
