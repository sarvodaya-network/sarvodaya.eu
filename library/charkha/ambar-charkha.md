# Ambar Charkha

The Ambar Charkha is the latest tool of nonviolent spinning developed in India. It comes with a degree of mechanisation, but it is still run by hand and does not require any kind of non-human power supply (but it can also be driven by solar power alternatively). 

* *Ambar* or *Ambara* stands for clothing in general and is used alternatively to *Vastra* (garments). Etymologically *Ambara* refers to cloth as well as to sky and cloud and for "bringing something together". 

* **Comparison to other Charkhas**: The regular spindle or *takli* is the most basic individual spinning tool. The regular, ancient *Charkha* is a simple spindle wheel making individual spinning faster and easier, that can be build by everyone and that has been in use from ancient times to modernity. The *Box-Charkha* was a development by Gandhi for mobility. Like carrying a suitcase one can carry ones spindle wheel around; the *Book-Charkha* brought the same into an even smaller and lighter form and they both directly integrated a *Winder*. They are still relatively simple to be made, but not as simply as the regular *Charkha*, they are therefore most often made in small manufactures. The Ambar Charkha is still enabling individual spinning, but is more complicated to be build. It requires some technical know-how, but it is still owneable and useable by individuals, which is the most important thing in regard to nonviolent production.  

* **Hand-Spinning vs. Hand-Driven Mechanism**: In difference to the former Charkhas, it enables one to fill two or more spindles simultaneously; it is run only by turning a wheel (or a food pedal for larger models). It means that the yarn itself is no longer spun by hand, but by a mechanism, and this mechanism is driven by hand. In this way, the yarn cannot be said to be fully hand-spun anymore, as the hand doesn't spin it, but it can still be considered as a non-violent means of spinning, because *Ambar Charkhas* can be produced in local manufactures, can be owned by individuals and used at home or in collective spinning workshops in every village. Therefore Vinoba appreciated the development as an expression of the wedding of nonviolence and science. 

* **Models**: Different models have been developed that are distinguished by the amounts of spindles filled simultaneously, such as:  
    * 2 spindle wheel
    * 4 spindle wheel 
    * 6 spindle wheel 
    * 8 spindle wheel 
    * 10 spindle wheel 
    * 12 spindle wheel 
    * 24 spindle wheel  

* **Winder** (Haspel): The Ambar Charkha, as the Box- and Book-Charkha too, has an integrated *winder* of 1m per turn. 

* **Versatility**: The Ambar Charkha can be used to spin Wool and Silk as well as Cotton.  

* **Preparation of Fibers:** For thick Cotton threads, short fibers are used. For thin Cotton threads, longer fibers are used. In order for the mechanism to work, the fiber has to be prepared in a specific way in order to make it useable, which is more work than the actual spinning process. Yarn can also be made from *roving*, "pre-yarn". That means, in order to spin with the ambar charkha, a thick, lose, even yarn has to be produced beforehand, that has to be loose enough to break when pulled, which is then feeded to the spindles to produce finer yarn.
