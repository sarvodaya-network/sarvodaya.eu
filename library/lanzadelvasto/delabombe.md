# About the Bomb

## The Problem of the Bomb or: Logical Decay

Physics teaches that nuclear decay is a work of reactions on the chain. The atomic problem, likewise, causes chain reactions in integrity of human reason and will and produces in nations a mental illness that attacks the very core of the faculties capable of solving it.  
Each piece of the bomb is a marvel of logic, knowledge, sagacity, foresight, invention, constructive skill: all this culminates in an explosion, senseless and disastrous.  
Likewise, each of the motives that led to its construction shows itself irresistible, each of the arguments that defend its necessity, irrefutable; the absurdity only breaks out in the conclusion.  
It is vain to shout that the accumulation of these devices is a mortal danger and a stupid crime, if we do not recognize the impeccable sequence of normal reactions, traditional reasons, concerns of prudence, which push men at this end.  
It's a logical stalker, it's a trick of the Devil. The most "clever" run getting caught in it.

It is undoubtedly unwise not to be able to oppose the neighboring powers than an inferior armament. It's probably even more reckless than not being not armed at all.  
What could be more reasonable than trying to catch up, if unfortunately we got ahead of himself?   
But those we have caught up with will not want to lose their lead, those that we have been ahead of will want to catch up with us, what could be more reasonable?  
What could be more reasonable than to say to yourself: if I own the absolute weapon, the neighbor struck with terror will reflect and I will be safe from his attack or rid of his
resistance?  
He will think, of course, but of what, if not of the means of procuring it also for
the same reasons?

Yes, but what could be more reasonable than to provide on both sides of "weapons of deterrence", as say in their excessively suave language our strategists and our policies? They also talk about the *balance of terror* and melt there‐on our safety.  
So therefore our thrifts so precise and strict about spending, our financiers so
attentive to the gain, our technicians so keen on performance, our statesmen always
trembling to find the budget in deficit, do not put into this business so many
millions and billions only to make it useless? What could be more reasonable,
isn't that right?  
It is the "balance of terror" that provides us with the last chance for peace,
isn't that right? But talking about the balance of terror is like talking about roundness
of the square or the whiteness of the black.  
It is written that "fear is the beginning of wisdom", it is true, yes, the fear of making a mistake, for example, or the fear of offending the neighbor, the fear of God, yes. But terror is the root of the most obscure follies.  
Considering that to this defenseless weapon, there is no other parade than to oppose fear
of his kind, everyone thinks of protecting himself by making himself threatening, and the more he threatens, the more he is threatened. It is a vicious circle, an aspiring whirlwind that leaves no other way out than death.
Each power that enters the vortex leads a chain of others to y enter, starting with the one he is most loath to see burst into his orbit: his worst enemy. And the more increases the number of nations endowed with the privilege to burst the earth, the more the risks multiply.  
That on a false news, on a misunderstanding, panic seizes the neighboring people or a slightly nervous head of state, that he strongly imagines that before midnight we are going to attack him by surprise, won't he try to hit us on first, and destroy us all at once?  
But if the belligerents resist the temptation of the first moment, will resist‐they to the one of the last one? Is it impossible that the one of the two who sees himself lost throws himself into a fit of desperate rage his supreme asset? Can we doubt for a moment that
Hitler, at the time when in his underground he shot himself in the temple and gave himself up to the flames, deprived himself of the dark joy of dragging the whole world into his downfall?

Once a British Minister of National Defense declared without bluntly, there is no possible defense to a nuclear attack. The only assurance he could give to the nation was that an automatic would immediately return to the opponent measure for measure. He concluded this historical speech thanking the population for taking it so well.  
There was something! because if I were to die sprayed, I don't know if this revenge
posthumous on millions of innocent people would console me.

Again, you will say, this is not about revenge, but about protection: the enemy, knowing that he cannot escape the response even if he annihilates us, is will keep from attacking us and the innocent will be safe on both sides.  
I'm not so sure about that. If the response depends on a delicate mechanism and on immediate and easy triggering, this device must be constantly under the supervision of some technicians. Let's admit that our enemy bribed one of them to cut the transmission wires, here he is sure (rightly or wrongly) that the riposte will not come and all the malice and the trick of our formidable equipment will be vain and paltry.  
It may also be that the triggering is done without malice, without trick and without betrayal, by simple technical incident. It may still be that a slight disruption of the mechanics make us hit on a peaceful neighbor, on our ally or on us‐same.  
Scribble is this village idiot who, to protect himself from the rain, put himself in the duck pond. Scribble was a forerunner. This is the thinking master of our strategists, our politicians, our valiant defenders, our leaders advanced.

But whatever may be expected and feared from the terror on the day when she will fall on the peoples, and from the crazy gesticulations that it will cause, nothing is more alarming today than the total lack of fear, indifference and callousness general.  
"Likewise, in the days of Noah," says Jesus, "people trafficked, married and were giving feasts, and the trickle of the great waters came upon them which swept them all away. »  
"Prophet, do not prophesy," they cry out as to Isaiah, "seeing, do not see : tell us pleasant things instead! »  
We look yawning at the images of Hiroshima, because Japan is so far away! The pleading appeals from Einstein and Schweitzer make people shrug their shoulders: what to do about it?  
The best thing to do is not to think about it, let's have fun soon!

Among other amusements, let's clap, with our mouths open and our noses in the air, at the
rockets against the sky, let's admire the wisdom of those who dream of inhabiting the moon after have made the earth uninhabitable. Let's hope from science and technology that they
we will make excellent vegetables, foreseeing the time when they will have poisoned everything that the earth produces in such a crude and primitive way.  
Let's listen to the wise politician who teaches us that the more bombs we will have in
reserve, the more peace we will have.  
Let us listen with confidence to the service scientist whose function consists in demonstrate that "all precautions have been taken to preserve the populations"; and especially the theologian who explains that all this is Orthodox, that we cannot find in Saint Thomas no argument that opposes nuclear weapons and that it it would be unwise to put forward objections to the doctrine of "just war".

When the explosion occurred in Hiroshima, it was a dazzling clarity and all the the center of the city was blown away in a flash.  
There arose from the city a wind so violent that it undressed the survivors. The women who were wearing kimonos found themselves naked with the drawings of the printed fabric on their flesh, decorated with burns. The wind machine-gunned the bodies with pricks of fire. Of hundreds of thousands were swallowed up in an instant, others put dozens of years to be consumed. To escape from the heated floor like the plate of an oven, some threw themselves into the river, but the water in the river was boiling.  
*The men will dry with terror because of the noise of the sea and the waves...* (Luke
21, 2‐5).  
The 500,000 tons of nitric acid that an H-bomb produces, the two million tons of dust that it raises, intercept the sunlight. A thousand one of them would hide it forever and make the earth a dead star.  
*And there will be signs in the sun, the moon and the stars... and the virtues of heaven will be shaken...* (Luke 21:25-27).  
*And there will be such tribulations in those days as from the beginning of the creation that God founded, until now, there have never been such great ones...* (Mark
13, 19).

"After all, some say, it may be God's will that the world perish," and they speak with a smiling serenity that would be truly sublime, if she came from a detachment from all things.  
But the people who take with such greatness of soul the end of everything in the Flood of Fire, are terrified at the thought of losing their place or displeasing their entourage or to pass for bad citizen (by protesting against the bomb, by example).  
Their imagination and common sense are so weak that they are unable even to animal fear. They go where we push them, like the cattle we take to the slaughterhouse, with this difference that they go there while philosophizing, with this difference again let no beast build the slaughterhouse, and forge the knife with which it is slaughtered.  
*So that they may have eyes not to see, ears not to hear...*  
*And God hardened Pharaoh's heart...*

## PRESTIGE, HONOR AND THE BOMB

It is true, we will be told, that the first atomic bomb destroyed all at once three hundred thousand people, but well over three hundred thousand were destroyed without scandal by non-atomic bombs in several hits. The number does nothing to the case.  
If we renounce the bomb, we must also renounce the cannon, the rifle, the sword. But then there will always be enough pebbles left to knock out the next one.  
And some will tell us this to demonstrate to us how much the company of disarming men, or more or less, is impossible and ridiculous, since the war is inevitable therefore necessary, even salutary and glorious, the others because that they look at war as a crime and find it impossible and ridiculous not to not demand total and immediate disarmament.  
To the latter we will answer that total and immediate disarmament cannot not be imposed and again this that, if by chance it could be imposed, it would remain inoperative, since not only can we kill and kill ourselves with stone, water, fire, the bread knife, the censer, but still with the tongue, with hatred and contempt, with indifference.  
If we renounce all weapons, we must first of all renounce the will to kill.  
Disarmament cannot therefore be the first step. This is the second one. The first not, it's the understanding (I don't say the agreement, I don't say love, to stick to the least requirement, below which there is nothing to do).  
Total disarmament would be such a disavowal of our habits and our millennial attitudes that we cannot think of accomplishing it at once: we must proceed little by little. It's already a lot to hope for a first step.  
The first step is obviously the one from which every reasonable being must recognize the necessity, and on which he cannot refuse to feel the urgency of the agreement, on pain of death.  
That's why we insist on the Bomb, which does not imply at all the approval of the other weapons.  

The one who announced "Whoever uses the sword will perish by the sword" did not wait the invention of the Bomb to condemn the war and show that it carries its own punishment.  
To others, to those who justify war, we will not dispute the strength of their main argument, which is that of the "legitimate defense". We will not dispute not to the man attacked by surprise at night the right to kill his attacker if he does not can't think of a better way to save his life.  
We will not dispute that the furious madman who shoots at all passers-by must be shot, if that's the only way to prevent it from harming. But what we let us affirm with force, it is that these are accidental cases, exceptional cases, limit cases, and we must be careful not to make examples of them and to draw general laws from them, and especially theories of legitimacy.  
Most human conflicts present themselves quite differently, give way to legal or moral solutions, in short, human, while rendering blow for blow until the stronger one is right about the other is of the order of the animal reflex and has only what to do with law and reason.  

In the event of war, who is the aggressor? Everyone says it's the other one. To such ensigns that today is called the Minister of War "Ministry of National Defense". "If everyone is defending themselves, where does the attack come from? ", asks Tolstoy. If we start justifying the defense, we will have to continue by justifying the attack as a preventive defense, or as a response to provocation, or as a revolt against the oppressor, or as a recovery of a good that was given to us snatched,  
Or to snatch from the neighbor a property that is necessary for us, or of which he has only
do, or that he has acquired badly,  
Or to defend our honor, fulfill our obligations, support our prestige,  
Or to divert the scandals of the regime, solve the question of the unemployment, diverting our enemies from within against the foreigner,  
To base peace on justice and justice on strength...

And while the chain of fury and horror lengthens, that of justifications double it and strengthens it.  
To which there is nothing to complain about, except that the reasons are too good. So Much
that there is no cause that is not justified, no atrocity that is not justified because of the justice of the cause. And this mass of justifications so well linked that we cannot to deny any completely covers the Fifth Commandment of God: YOU DO NOT WILL NOT KILL  
which was given on a stone table and without margins, so that we could not no comments.  
Also their comment, they did not put it next to or below, but at the‐on it, and, in place of the command, there are now all the teachings and recommendations to the contrary.  
As a result of which the war comes like a plague. And it is a plague made of man's hand.  
And everyone knows that this is God's punishment.  
To what do we recognize the punishment of God? To this the culprit applies it to himself
zealously, relentlessly, and adjusts it himself to his case.  

*To the sequence of legitimate violence*, those who find their justification *in the wrongs of the opponent*, there are two ways out and there are only two:  
Or *perpetual war*, as history proves; and now, with the advent of total war and the absolute weapon, total destruction.  
Or *the breaking of the chain*, or *liberation*, or *conversion*, or even *Non‐
Violence*.  
The one that the Gospel preaches and, five centuries earlier, Buddha, and ten centuries before
Buddha, Joseph, son of Jacob Israel; one of whom a continuous tradition of saints, of wise men, prophets set the spiritual conditions, the inner dispositions.  
While in our century in extreme peril, Gandhi showed the applications practical.  
Shown that this resistance by the Strength of the Spirit (for it is indeed of power and struggle that it is about, not resignation and acquiescence) is capable of repel an invader, even if he were a thousand times better armed, even if he were installed in the country for more than a century.  
Able to raise up a class of oppressed, as the liberation of the outcasts.  
Able in a few days to end a war, since the massacres Hindus and Pakistanis were arrested net.  
Justice demands that we fight for her to the death, but these historical examples pave the way for other forms of combat and teach that the use of war, doubtful at all times, today disastrous, is now *useless*.  

That said, and the bottom of my thought confessed in two words, I return to the first point, I mean to the bomb, and meet those who do not want to follow me to the bout, who stick to the reasonable and traditional argument that guns are justified by the need for war and war by the need for defense.  
Was it true – and we could well believe it in the time of Mac Mahon and even of Joffre and Foch –, it still remains that nuclear weapons are specifically different from all the others. The amount of casualties and damage does not enter alone in the line of account. It is critical points, passages to the limit where the quantitative difference affects the very quality of things and the nature of problems.  

If war is acceptable up to a certain point as a defense, as lesser evil, as an advantage over the immediate and certain evil that it does, of the good superior as we hope, armament, likewise, is acceptable only in a balance between offensive and defensive weapons.  
The Middle Ages, a time called barbaric, but a time of legend and epic, has for character a remarkable superiority of defensive weapons.  
The offensive weapons are almost as rudimentary there (their shape and decoration miles apart) than those of cave time: slingshots, bows and arrows, maces, axes, swords, spears, that's about it.  
But what technical wonders and what inventive genius in defense! Cotte of meshes, complete breastplate of the man and the horse, helmet with visor, gauntlets articulated, shield: movable carapace on the carapace; and then walls, double and triple curtains, ditches, plessis, drawbridge, nail grilles, metal-plated doors, watchtowers, towers, battlements, chicanes, barbacanes, machicolations, and the keep and the underground galleries.  
The knight is almost invulnerable, climbing the ramparts often impossible. It is necessary to reduce citadels and castles by starvation or to take them to betrayal. Such a historical battle ended in a few dozen deaths. The overthrown enemy was taken prisoner, released for ransom, and often freed on my word. The war kept a tournament look, sometimes deadly game, but by accident, test of valor and self-control.  
With the cannon and the musket, the predominance of offensive weapons is blamed (and immediately the chivalric virtues fall into disuse and discredit). The breastplate is getting high, the walls are crumbling. The man ends up presenting himself to the machine gun the chest uncovered, having for defense only the chance to pass between the bailes and the balls, for more than the rank of those who precede it.

The First World War marks the extreme limit of the renunciation of all defense, if not the most basic, which is to hide out, to throw yourself flat on your stomach in the holes and folds of the ground.  
The waves of assault advance, making rampart and ramp of hundreds of thousands of corpses.  
At the end, we see a precarious helmet reappear to keep stray bullets, falling shards and pebbles, and, in the last few months, assault tanks to force the trenches.  
Between the two wars, a whole defensive equipment arose, based on false calculations, on outdated strategic theories. The Maginot Line is getting better, or rather goes underground. But from the first push, this new Wall of China background, sand castle at the tide; the air attack with its parachuted troops he steps over it while playing himself and takes it backwards.  
The same was true in their time of the blockhouses of the Atlantic Wall and the Siegfried Line.  
Much more, the entire front of the armies constitutes only a very protective imperfect hinterland, prone to air incursions and long-range fire scope.  
We could oppose the so-called "passive" defense, which is to lock ourselves in the cellars, to shoot at the planes or to give chase to them.  
Against rockets, nothing like this will be effective, and even underground shelters will not
will not protect us from atomic bombing, if it permanently poisons the surfaces where it will be necessary to draw air and sustenance.  

The sword, noble weapon.  
It is offensive by the single point, and by everything else defensive.  
The Bomb is the dastardly weapon and defended by definition, since without parade.  
If the legitimacy of the struggle is the defense, the Absolute Weapon that is without parade
is any offense and absolutely bad.  

What is absolutely bad is also absurd.  
It is conceivable that a man sacrifices himself for his land and for his home, but if he
sacrifices at the same time what he sacrifices himself for, there is no longer sacrifice, but suicide and crime without forgiveness.  
To die in nuclear war is to die three times: to die yourself, to die in his children, to die with the whole of nature.  

Ah, my friends! look up, look up quickly to the sky while it is still blue. Touch the earth before it crumbles. Run to the source to drink before that we poison it, you bathe in the sea before it is infested. But especially watch your children play before they fall into languor, before their blood turns, before they burn slowly.  
You are afraid that they will get their feet wet, poor little ones! You are afraid let them catch cold. You are afraid that they will miss their exam, poor children!  
But from the learned wounds that the atom chipmakers are preparing for them, you don't worry, do you?

We are at a turning point in history where France could have played its role.   
We do not believe in the "nuclear vocation of France", we believe in its chivalrous vocation.  
The "means of mass destruction" are the negation of all virtue chivalrous.  
Exterminate the enemy mechanically, from afar and without even having seen him, men, women, old men indiscriminately, it is the opposite of all justice, of all honor and all glory.  
It is indeed about prestige! Cold-cooking this huge crime is the hallmark of the greater cowardice and baseness! Cunning inspired by terror – and for that matter cunning blind and who catches himself in his own trap  
Blessed are the peoples who do not have this temptation, and even more so those who know how to
resist out of greatness of soul or simply out of common sense!  
Those who don't have the bomb are also the ones who are most likely to be preserved, whatever one may say.  
Because if a conflict breaks out between two owners of nuclear weapons, it will be fatally nuclear (the heads of state have declared this unequivocally).  
But if a conflict breaks out between a power with and without weapons nuclear weapons, it is likely that the armed power will stick to conventional weapons, as was the case in the Korean War, although a decisive victory could not be obtained.  
It is all the more likely that the opponent will be more weakly armed.  
The most ambitious and inhuman of the conquerors cannot find the slightest interest in ruling over disintegrated peoples, in annexing radioactive deserts.  
To talk about conquest as well as defense with atomic devices is to make evidence of outdated turns of mind is to believe oneself in the good old days of Déroulède.  

The future is for peoples without a Bomb. They represent, moreover, the majority, the
bottom, the reserve.  
Will they have the lucidity to realize that it is an honor, an advantage, and a insurance? Will they be able to stand up to the Disintegrators and dissuade them?  
Who can speak on their behalf, defend them, unite them, cover himself in their eyes from the
glory of a savior, if not the one who could possess the absolute weapon, but refuses it?  
The one who owns it and renounces it.  
This is the leading role that France could have played and that it could still to be taken at the cost of renouncing senseless and harmful claims.  
Will she continue to push herself between Tall, pot of earth between two pots of iron, arouse defiance and hatred on all sides, rush into the work, setbacks and dangers, for the sole purpose of claiming the right to have its share to the Great Destruction?  
Or will she show herself to be smart enough, free enough, brave enough to assume this mission which would make her praised and blessed by the whole world and the future generations?
