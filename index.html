What is Sarvodaya?

Sarvodaya means the "Welfare of All", the "Good of All" and describes the vision of a non-violent social order, based on spiritual principles, as proposed and strived for by Gandhi, Vinoba, Lanza Del Vasto and other non-violent revolutionaries of the last century. While it has originated in India, the philosophy of Sarvodaya has been heavily influenced by other streams of thought as well, such as by Ruskin, Thoreau and Tolstoj. As such it also has much in commom with non-violent anarchist ideas as proposed by Pjotr Kropotkin and Gustav Landauer. The means by which Sarvodaya is meant to be achieved is via "Satyagraha", the non-violent fight, concretely expressed in form of the "Constructive Programme". Essential parts of the Constructive Programme (in so far as they are relevant to the whole world and not just in regard to the more country-specific areas it contains, are:

  • Basic Non-Violent Education focused on craft, practice and the development of independence in regard to self-rule, self-sufficiency and self-thinking.
  • Decentralisation of Power via Lok-niti, Peoples Rule (instead of nominal Democracy driven by Party Politics) to achieve local Self-Sovereignty, including the Shanti Sena (Peace Army/Force) as a non-violent force of workers for the establishment and keeping of non-violent order.
  • Decentralisation of Economy through local Self-Sufficiency, Small Machines owned by individual craftsmen or local collectives, anti-corporate local production for local needs, and Swadeshi (always supporting the local craftsmen first), with the ultimate goal of liberation from money through mutual support, sharing and service.
  • Non-violent/anti-abusive informational order and self-thinking, through a decentralosed, anti-fragile infrastructure for digital Independence, Security and Privacy.

The Sarvodaya Network is an attempt to create an international open source network of ressources and communication platforms revolving around Sarvodaya and all its above mentioned aspects and related streams of thought. We try to preserve books about the history of Sarvodaya and its diverse constructive programmes and attempt to foster new ideas and approaches for the demands of our time.
