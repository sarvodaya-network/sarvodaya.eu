The constructive programme is a decidedly apolitical programme for building up a non-violent social order, that can be taken up by anyone at any time. It is serving individual and local needs, tackling both individual, as well as regional and international problems directly from the bottom up, without party-politics and political intervention.

Structure Idea:  
I. Introduction / Philosophy of Life
II. Internal Work / Spiritual/Moral Foundation or "source".  
III. External Work / The Programme deduced from or flowing from the source.
IV. Towards Total Revolution - the ocean that the programme runs into.  


## Aim and Means of the Programme 

**Harmony of Interests:**  
The aim of the programme is *Sarvodaya*, the good of all, the vision of a non-violent social order. We hold that the good of one is contained in the good of all and the good of all is contained in the good of one. There can be no conflict of interests, as the true interests of each and everyone are in harmony with the interests of everyone else. 

**Harmony of Means and Ends:**  
As one always harvests what one sows, the means of the programme are strictly non-violent. Non-violent means are not just a possible mode to choose for the achievement of a non-violent goal, it is the only possible means, as the goal is contained in the means.  

**Harmony of Inner and Outer Life:**  
In the past, spiritual minds have usually neglected outer life and social practice. And revolutionary minds have usually neglected inner life and spiritual practice. We are uniting inner and outer life. Thus the constructive programme is at the same time spiritual and revolutionary work and one through the other. We strive for total revolution without and total liberation within.  
> The purification of the self and the change of society are not only not mutually exclusive, but are inter-related and interdependent.  
> The individual search for truth must end in social change and the medium of social change should be individual search for truth. (Narayan Desai)  

**Harmony of local and international needs:**  
Every individual is humanity in essence, and every locality is the world in essence.  
In order to solve the problems of all, we have to solve the problems of the individual. Thus we must begin with ourselves.  
In order to solve the problems of the world, we have to solve the problems of the village, city and neighbourhood. Thus we must begin with the construction of non-violence where we are.  
We must therefore *"think globally, act locally."* Which we also could reformulate as such: "Think universally, act individually." Or in yet other terms we could say with Gandhi: "Be yourself the change you want to see in the world." This is all that the constructive programme is about.  


## Organisations

The Constructive Programme is free and independent from any formal organisation or institution.  
The programme is put into practice by a free association consisting of independent individuals and small local circles without any binding, central organisation, determined only by shared spiritual principles and the most fundamental ideas of non-violence as applied to the pedagogical and socio-economic field. All that is holding us together are the principles and the virtue they demand, summarised in the nine precepts. <!-- These ideas are our only weapons and our only protection. -->

We might say: Humans of the world, unite! But one cannot unite to oppress; in total union there can be no oppression, because in Sarvodaya no one is left out, its union is the union with the Absolute; a union of all or nothing. We must unite, and for this we might have to organise, but we must not depend on anything or anyone to act.

This rejection of any binding, central organisation does not exclude the formation of even strict organisations for service or the fulfillment of particular missions in specific localities, regions or lands (such as the *Sarva Seva Sangh* in India or the *Shanti Sena*); it only excludes the existence of any organisation *above* the level of the principle and any kind of formal authority *over* the principle. The principle is our only binding authority. Only the principle itself can be the guiding force and there cannot exist any kind of institution or authority above the principle (of truth, non-violence); therefore there can also not exist any kind of institution *representing* the principle. 

The principle or ideas can be represented by anyone. Any man on the street is as capable to represent it as any other or any organisation and so the constructive program can be put into practice by everyone who strives to live accordingly. One can *always* recognise them *by their deeds*. Deeds in discord with the principle of truth and non-violence by any individual or any institution are thus never disproving the principle, the program or the movement; they are always only proving those doing so as acting against the principle, the program and the movement (if done willingly; or, when unwillingly, it shows their weakness or incapability of acting accordingly).    

Any organisation founded to support our cause, must comply to the fundamental principles of non-violent organisation:  

1. It must be *non-violent* to the best of its ability, it must enforce nothing and do and support nothing that threatens peace.  
2. It must not only be non-profit, non-commercial, it must be *anti-commercial*.  
3. It must make decisions based on *consensus*
4. Every part of it, even to the individual level, must be *autonomous*; it must thus be organised in decentralised ways from the bottom up, with full autonomy at the local and individual level and mere moral authority at the centre that only gives advise.
5. It must be strictly *non-partisan*. Who wants to serve the Good of All and proclaims the harmony of interests, can not do any party politics or support any party over another. It is for the service of all, in the interest of all, even if focusing on a particular need or a particular group in need at a time.   


## The Programme is based on Labour

The constructive programme is primarily focused on labour, on economical service, because labour is literally the "life foundation" of everyone, as it is labour that produces everyone's needs. Whoever controls the "life foundation" of the people, controls the people.  
Corporations control people by continueously taking more and more economical areas into their hands. They try to control and rule the people by going so far as to even take illusionary property of seeds, a crime and lie beyond measure.  
If whoever who controls the life foundation of the people controls the people, then it follows, that it is the most important thing to give the control over their life foundation back to the people. Therefore it is this what the constructive programme is primarily focussing on. It has to make sure that the people become self-reliant and independent in their sustenance, in real ways, not in illusionary ways. Earning money in itself doesn't make one a iota more independent nor more self-reliant in any meaningful way.  
Therefore, the foundation of the constructive programme is the foundation of our life:  
The purification and liberation of our livelihood, of our food, cloth and shelter, summarised as *village industry*.  
This is where everything starts and this is what most constructive workers will always be focused on doing mostly.

## The Nine Precepts 

At the time of Gandhiji he has coined eleven precepts. They were based on the five precepts of Satya, Ahimsa, Aparigraha, Asteya and Brahmacharya from the ancient traditions, that the Hindu tradition shares with the Jainas and Buddhists. Gandhi added six additional precepts to them based on the demands of our time, but while some of them are of universal value, at least one of them had a particular Hindu context that isn't applicable to the international level.   
Vinobaji had added a twelfth precept to the eleven. The 9 precepts presented here are a simplification only by form; they contain *all* of the precepts by Gandhiji and Vinobaji and above that they also contain all *ten* of the buddhist precepts, as a matter of gradual refinement. I explain this in more detail in an article on its own.  

1. Truth                        / liberation from ignorance/delusion
2. Nonviolence                  / liberation from violence
3. Internal Training            / liberation from desire
4. External Labour              / liberation from abuse/exploit.
5. Non-possession               / liberation from property
6. Synthetical Thinking         / liber. from confusion and viol. thought
7. Self-Rule or Autonomy        / liber. from the state
8. Self-Sufficiency or Autarky  / liber. from harmful dependency
9. Selfless Service             / liber. from money

We could summarise the precepts in prosaic form, as such:

We believe in the absolute value of *truth* & *nonviolence*  
aspired through *internal training* and *physical labour*  
on the basis of *non-possession* and *synthetical thinking*  
via gradual practice of *autonomy* and *self-sufficiency*  
expressed in selfless *service* without desire.  

All of the nine precepts are basically included in truth and non-violence and are nothing but logical inferences of them; truth and non-violence themselves are logical inferences of each other. Therefore the fundamental insistence is on them; if they are understood in their evidence and applied to all aspects of life, the other precepts are following on their own; their necessity can then not be denied, as it becomes evident that they are necessary to express truth and non-violence in the respective domains of life. At the same time, the precepts can be paired in different ways to reveal their interconnection, their meaning and mutual support.  

What is not true, is violent. And what is violent, is not true. As truth and non-violence are logical inferences of each other, there is a term that is including both of them: *Satyagraha*, short *truthistence*, the insistence on truth, the non-violent fight for truth, or the truthful fight for non-violence. If you do not like the word fight, then call it aspiration, search, experimentation, struggle, striving.    
Truth and nonviolence can never be taken apart. Any commitment of violence is proving a degree of untruth. And every untruth is proving some degree of violence.  
*Satyagraha* expresses more than the inference of truth through nonviolene and vice versa; it also expresses the fundamental truth of the indestructible link between means and ends, which once will be accepted as a fundamental part of the rule of cause and effect: a violent action can never result in a peaceful outcome. Who takes the sword will die by the sword.

**Truth** (*Satya*): Not to lie nor to deceit, not others nor oneself. To speak the truth, search for the truth and to accept truth wherever it appears; it doesn't matter who speaks the truth nor who lies. We won't accept any untruth from anyone no matter his authority or position and in the same way we will not reject any truth from anyone no matter his authority or position, as truth is the only authority with actual (and righteous) coercive power.  

Adherence to Truth automatically implies and requires an open mind. A mind that is not open is closed and into a closed mind, truth cannot enter. Keeping ones mind closed implies that one has no need for any kind of new truth to enter, no need for any refinement of ones view, that one either has all truth already or has no need for truth.  
* A closed mind is also a sign of arrogance, whereas an open mind is a sign of humility.   
* A closed mind is also an unfree mind, because the closeness does neither allow truth to enter nor to get out. An open mind is a free mind as truth can freely be entered and freely be expressed.   
* An open mind is a mind that is capable to constantly change, whereas a closed mind is petrified.

But openness is not just a requirement for truth to enter; it is also the nature of truth, as truth itself is open; it is closed off only to those who close themselves, as truth doesn't force itself unto anyone. 

Becoming open means to not hold on to anything, to let everything flow in and out at will without tainting you, without holding on; it is thus the positive aspect of what in negative terms is called detachment, or in Eckhart terms, *Abgeschiedenheit*. Thus, an open mind is also a detached mind. 



**Nonviolence** (*Ahimsa*): In negative terms in its most basic application it means never to deliberately kill; in its subtler application it means to never deliberately harm or hurt anyone. In its positive application it can be summarised in the christian doctrine of "love each other", whereas *respect* is its most basic minimum, whereas the love of our neighbour, which is anyone and not particularly those we are naturally inclined to like, but especially those we are not inclined to like and in particular the love of our *enemies* is the most difficult and nonetheless necessary aspect of it. 

**Internal Training** (*Brahmacharya*): With internal or spiritual training we mean what in India is called *Brahmacharya*, which fundamentally is about detachment (from ones body and mind) and which can be practiced to different degrees. A minor degree of Sense restraint and the control of ones palate (Asvada) we treat as basic aspects of it. In its most strict application it would amount to a high degree of sense restraint, which includes celibacy; it also includes, in buddhist terms, the practice of no-distraction, no-beautification and of not sleeping on high, luxurious beds. In short, it can as well be called *Ascesis*, which we would use if it wouldn't been so misunderstood, as it does neither mean self-mortification nor penance; it means exercise, it means training oneself internally.  
Brahmacharya is a commitment to truth and non-violence, and the acknowledgement that comitting to truth and non-violence requires constant effort. The *necessity* of internal training arises from the acknowledgement of our own imperfection, in other words, it arises from the acknowledgement of untruth and violence in us, the acknowledgement of a need for internal purification which requires *work*. Not understanding the necessity of this work is always a form of ignorance, which is either a form of lying, deceiving oneself about ones own perfection, not requiring any further work, or a misunderstanding or lack of knowledge in regard to the possibility of such work and purification. In any way it implies ignorance in regard to the violence of oneself and within oneself, proven by a lack of internal peace, proven by a lack of external peace. 

**Bread Labour** (Sharira-Shrama): Just as the commitment to truth and non-violence requires internal work on ourselves, it also requires external work. While we are living in a body, as embodied beings, we require food for sustenance, we clad our body in cloth and shelter it from cold and rain. As long as we live, we have physical needs. These physical needs must be satisfied in order to survive and in order to do anything else, including the internal training that brings us closer to the truth. Physical needs can only ever be met by physical labour. And when I do not physical labour for my physical needs, no matter what else I do, it inevitably means that others have to do physical labour for my needs. When anyone works for *me* instead of with me it means that either I don't do what I should do or that I have more demands than I should have. All social abuse begins in the acceptance of this condition and the avoidance of physical labour that my needs demand from me. So it is not about simply physical labour nor even about productive labour, since production today has little to do with our needs; bread labour is about physical labour for actual *needs*, it is about substinence, about sustainable livelihood. And surely there is no need to do everything oneself; it is not without a reason that people cooperate and share their knowledge and capabilities. But it will always remain a lie that physical labour and the value of physical labour can be replaced or even compared to any kind of other activity. As long as I am physically able to do productive physical labour for the sustenance of my own body and its needs, I ought to do that if I can.    

**Non-Possession** (Aparigraha): It is simple to see, that the very idea of ownership is not justified from the start. One aspect of Non-Possession is Non-Stealing. We subsume it under this. Why? Because I cannot steal anything if not for the underlying idea of ownership and the idea of taking ownership of something that isn't given to me. But every kind of ownership started in taking something that wasn't given, or at least - that wasn't given to me exclusively. Every ownership of land did start by some people *taking* land that wasn't theirs and explaining it to belong to them and them alone. This was a lie to start with and violent too; it simply isn't true. So there is no ownership of land, can't be. Anything else that men has produced is produced from the land too; he never made anything from nothing, he always only made things from other things that he has taken or that were given to him *for free* and never for him exclusively, but to everyone; that is what all ressources are. The principle of Non-Possession is not about getting rid of all things; although within the gradual training, voluntary poverty, externally and internally, is the goal and it is always wise to start going into this direction sooner than later, for the simple fact that in death we will leave behind everything anyway. But it begins with the simple acknowledgement, that we are not the owners of whatever we have. We are trustees. We have them now at our disposal, but neither is it any truthful or non-violent to consume blindly ever, while others suffer lack, no matter who something belongs to, nor it is justified to exclude others who may be more in need than I am, from partaking in what I have for whatever reason, while I do not need as much. This turns it into a simple principle, applicable by everyone, no matter how rich or poor he externally is. 

**Synthetical Thinking** (Samanvaya): When Non-Possession is a particular relation to external things, then Samanvaya is a particular relation to thought. Here too, we avoid any exclusivity. Thoughts do not belong to us. They belong to all. We should freely share thought and we should freely accept thought from everywhere and everyone. If there is any truth in anything, we should accept it. That includes the readiness to study material from all kinds of directions and an active attempt to find a common ground; to find the truth in them and where the truth aligns, or where two smaller truths can expand into a higher truth. It is an integral form of thinking, a constant renewal of thought based on the eternal principles that are to be found anywhere, and education along these lines. 

7. Swarajya

**Swadeshi:** Whereas Bread labour is primarily a particular relation to our own livelihood, with a direct relation to our own and an indirect relation to that of others, swadeshi is primarily a particular relation to the livelihood of others, with an indirect relation to that of ourselves. Whereas Bread labour is a discipline of work of our own in order to not harm others or burden others too much with our needs, preventing abuse on my behalf, Swadeshi is a discipline of mutual support through strict adherence to the principle of supporting those in closer proximity over those further away; which is preventing abuse on behalf of others. At the same time, whereas Swarajya is political independence expressed in local self-rule or autonomy, swadeshi is economical independence, expressed in local self-sufficiency or autarky. 

9. Seva

> So konstituieren (1) die Philosophie, die das Wohl Aller zu verwirklichen trachtet, (2) das konstruktive Programm und (3) die Übung der Gelübde unterstützt durch Hingabe unsere dreifältige Vision des Lebens.

to directly derive the constructive program FROM the precepts; in this way we could deeply establish the connection.
Because otherwise, people may be interested in the external work only and miss doing the necessary internal work.

1. Internal (also called "spiritual") liberation: As social equality and external peace can only be achieved on the basis of individual equanimity of mind and peace within, our program is based on internal training, also called "ascesis". Practice of *Virtue*, *Sense Restraint*, *Sacrifice* (yajna), *Giving* (dana) are practiced on the individual level first, then applied to the social context. While it will always keep occuring that some will try to work on other parts of the program while not considering this internal training as necessary, it is the very ground on which everything else grows. You can of course throw these seeds around, but they will not fall on fertile ground. The "effectiveness" of our programme depends on the purification of ourselves. 
2. Liberation of Thinking through what we call a *Discipline of Thought*. This includes learning, sharing of ideas among individuals, small study circles, distribution of ideas in spoken and written form and in a more concrete sense, education. *Nai Talim* ("New Education") is Educating through Craft, Gardening & Agriculture, including digital education for digital freedom. Because Education is by definition life-long, it is not just pedagogics; it is a holistic process in which everyone can partake at any time and till the end. 
3. Economical Liberation by local or regional *autarky* through local collectives, the revival of craft, individual or collective ownership of the means of production, decentralisation of wealth through trusteeship (*Vishvastavritti*), non-possession (also called "deliberate poverty", which begins in *sufficiency* as the minimum), *Swadeshi* (discipline of regionality, economy for the good of ones neighbour), *Khadi* (handmade clothing), overcoming the ownership of land through *Bhudan* (landgifts), approaching the housing problem<!-- (including local self-build huts and yurts adjusted to local needs)--> through *gramdan* and *kutidan*, low tech, small high-tech, shared use and repair; with *Kanchan-Mukti* (liberation from money) as the final step.  
4. Political Liberation: Swaraj through Gram-Swaraj in form of *Lok-Niti* (Peoples Power, instead of a nominal democracy driven by Party Politics) through the establishment of local councils and the *Shanti Sena* (Peace Army) maintained through the *Sarvadaya Patra*, with the ultimate goal of liberation from the state (*Danda-Mukti*) or Coercive Authority.  



We do not count fearlessness among the precepts for a similar reason for which we do not count humility among the precepts. Meister Eckhart says: (why Demut cannot be counted among them). Humility is the basis of all virtue. And fearlessness is the result of virtue; they are not something that one can easily vow to be. Humility and fearlessness are the result of applicating the precepts. All the precepts are about something to be actively done or something to actively not-do; humility and fearlnessness on the other hand are a particular mode. Humility is a way of relating to oneself. Fearlessness is a way of relating to other beings and things. They are also the two sides of one coin of detachment. Total detachment would automatically result in total humility and total fearlessness.
